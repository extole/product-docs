# Overview

Most programs start as a copy of a template, as [Creating a Program](/guides/platform-overview/creating-a-program)
describes. When no template has the flow you need, you build the marketing campaign from
components. [Configuration Concepts](/technical/configuring-extole/configuration-concepts)
explains what a component is and how you work with one. This page gives Extole's conventions for
laying a marketing campaign out, and what the platform evaluates for you.

One convention shapes the rest: **your systems send plain events, and the campaign does the
counting.** Send one event each time a person does something, with the identifiers of the people
and records involved. Put every count, time window, total, and limit in the campaign, where it
shows in Flow Builder and changes without a release of your systems. Do not ask your systems for a
running count, a total, or an event that says a person qualified.

To build the campaign with the Management API, see
[Build a Campaign from Components Over REST](/technical/platform-integrations/rest-apis/build-a-campaign-from-components-over-rest).
To build it in Flow Builder, see
[How to Add a New Business Event](/guides/flow-campaigns/how-to-add-a-new-business-event).

## Map the Program to the Campaign

| In the program | In the campaign | In the templates |
|---|---|---|
| Each kind of person | A role | `advocate` and `friend` in Refer A Friend, `participant` in Reward for Action |
| Each stage a person moves through | A journey in that role | A marketing journey for the promotion, and a journey for the actions that follow |
| Each action a person takes | A business event in that journey | `shared`, `signed_up`, `converted` |
| Each thing a person earns | A reward on the role of the person who earns it | Advocate Reward on Friend Converted |

- **One business event for each action**, even an action that only counts toward a later
  condition. Rules read the business events that the campaign records.
- **Record every action, whatever its amount.** Put a threshold, such as a minimum order value, on
  the rule that counts the actions, such as **Value is greater than** on **Has Taken Action**, and
  not on the business event that records the action. The smaller actions then still show in
  reports.
- **Name the business event after the action.** The name is the event name on the person's
  profile and in reports.
- **Put the business event in the role of the person who acts.** A reward for someone else reaches
  that person through a published event, as
  [Reward One Person for Another Person's Actions](#reward-one-person-for-another-persons-actions)
  describes.
- **Put each rule where its question is asked.** A journey's targeting decides who takes part. A
  business event's trigger rules decide when an event counts. A reward's rules decide whether the
  person qualifies.

## Order the Flow as the Events Happen

A campaign reads from top to bottom in the order its events take place. A program in which one
person brings in another runs in this order:

1. The first person sees and clicks the promotion: `promotion_viewed` and `promotion_clicked`, in a
   marketing journey.
2. The first person takes the action that brings them into the program.
3. The first person shares the invitation: `shared`, in a copy of the `share` journey.
4. The second person clicks the invitation: `share_clicked`, in a copy of the `new_customer`
   journey.
5. The second person takes the actions that count.
6. The first person's role records what those actions give back, in a later journey, and the
   rewards follow it.

A program for one person uses steps 1, 2, and 6. Keep the promotion and the invitation even when
the program description leaves them out: the promotion records how a person found the program,
and the share link ties the second person to the first. **Publish Event Data** uses that referral
relationship to find the first person, so the second person's events need no identifier for the
first person.

Flow Builder draws each role as a column and each journey as a row. Rows run in the order of each
journey's `sequence` setting, across all the roles, and the business events of a journey run in
the order of their own `sequence`. Number the journeys in the order of the flow, each with a
number of its own. Refer A Friend numbers the advocate's marketing journey 1, the advocate's share
journey 2, and the friend's journey 4. A role that acts at two points of the flow has a journey for
each point.

## Give Each Part What It Needs to Run

The Custom Campaign Template holds one role, one journey, and one business event, and no reward.
Every template that runs a program adds these parts, and your campaign needs them too:

| Part | What it needs | Guide |
|---|---|---|
| Business event | One trigger: the input event, zone, or published event that starts it | [How to Set Up Triggered By](/guides/flow-campaigns/how-to-set-up-triggered-by) |
| Journey | A targeting scenario, which decides who enters the journey | [How to Target a Program](/guides/flow-campaigns/how-to-target-a-program) |
| Reward | The business event that earns it, a reward supplier, and a reward email | [How to Add a New Reward](/guides/flow-campaigns/how-to-add-a-new-reward) |
| Reward | Reward rules for each condition on the reward | [How to Set Up Reward Rules](/guides/flow-campaigns/how-to-set-up-reward-rules) |

A new reward arrives with two reward rules: **Annual Reward Value Limit**, set to a total of 2,000
in a tax year that starts on January 1, and Business Event Quality, which passes when the earning
event's quality is high. A copied journey arrives with its own rules too: the `share` journey brings
a limit on share emails, a limit on mass sharing, self-referral prevention, and a country list on
its targeting. Keep each rule your program needs, set it, and remove the others.

Give every reward a supplier from the start, even before your fulfillment exists. A custom reward
that Extole fulfills and sends by itself works as a placeholder: a test person earns the reward
and receives the reward email, and you replace the supplier when your fulfillment is ready.
[Build a Campaign from Components Over REST](/technical/platform-integrations/rest-apis/build-a-campaign-from-components-over-rest#use-a-placeholder-supplier-until-fulfillment-is-connected)
has the settings.

## What Extole Evaluates

| The program says | Put it in the campaign as |
|---|---|
| "Counts once for each order, account, or membership" | A unique event key on the business event's data. Extole records the business event once for each key value. See [Unique Event Keys](/technical/troubleshooting/duplicate-action-alerts-and-unique-event-keys#unique-event-keys). |
| "An order of \$40 or more" | **Value is greater than** on **Has Taken Action**, set to 40. It includes the number you set. **Event Value Greater Than** passes only above its number. |
| "Purchases add up to \$1,500" | **Has Taken Action** as a reward rule, with **Total value is greater than** set to 1500 and a **Recent Activity Window**. The total includes the event being evaluated and the number you set. |
| "Only after the person did something else" | **Has Taken Action** as a trigger rule on the business event. See [Common Trigger Rules](/guides/flow-campaigns/common-trigger-rules). |
| "In this order" | **Has Taken Action** on the earlier action, as a trigger rule on the later action's business event. It passes when the earlier action is on the profile, whatever its date, so it checks the order in which the events arrive. Test it with each case's events sent oldest first. |
| "Count only what they do after joining" | **Has Taken Action** on the joining business event, as a trigger rule on the counted business event. An action before joining is then not recorded, so no count or total includes it. |
| "At least 30 days after joining" | **Minimum Action Age** on that trigger rule. The earlier event counts only when it is at least that old. |
| "Within 30 days" | **Recent Activity Window** on **Has Taken Action**. |
| "Six sessions in the same calendar month" | An **Expression Reward Rule** that counts the events dated in the calendar month of the event being evaluated. See [Count by Calendar Month](#count-by-calendar-month). |
| "Three months in a row" | The same **Expression Reward Rule**, which also checks each of the months before. See [Count by Calendar Month](#count-by-calendar-month). |
| "For each child, up to two a month" | The same **Expression Reward Rule**, which counts each child's events apart and passes for no more than two children in a calendar month. See [Count by Calendar Month](#count-by-calendar-month). |
| "Within a day of another action" | **Has Taken Action** on the other action, with a **Recent Activity Window** of hours, as a trigger rule on the later action's business event. A window counts hours back from the event, so it approximates a calendar day. |
| "At least 150 days since the last one", where each one restarts the clock | A business event that the counted business event triggers, with **Has Taken Action** on the counted business event, a **Recent Activity Window** of 150 days, and **Exact Count** 1. The count includes the event that triggered it, so 1 means no other one in the window. |
| "Between 150 and 240 days since the last one" | The rule above, and beside it **Has Taken Action** on the counted business event with a **Minimum Action Age** of 150 days and a **Recent Activity Window** of 240 days. |
| "At least five times" | **Minimum Count** on **Has Taken Action**, as a reward rule. |
| "On the third time" | A business event that the counted business event triggers, with **Has Taken Action** on the counted business event and **Exact Count** 3 as a trigger rule. The count includes the event that triggered it. Put the reward on the new business event. |
| "Every three, and each one counts once" | The same business event, with two trigger rules: **Has Taken Action** with **Exact Count** listing 3 and each multiple of it, such as 3, 6, 9 and on up to the most a person could reach, and no window, so it passes only when a new set of three is complete; and **Has Taken Action** with **Minimum Count** 3 and the **Recent Activity Window**, so the set falls in the window. The sets run over the person's whole history, so three actions in the window earn only when they complete a set, and a set that spans more than the window earns nothing and is still used up. |
| "Unless they cancel" | **Has Not Taken Action**, with the business event that disqualifies the person. |
| "Unless they cancel during the waiting period" | **Has Not Taken Action** as a reward rule, with a **Recent Activity Window** at least as long as the **Pending Period**. When the period ends, Extole runs the reward's rules again, so a cancellation during the period stops the reward. |
| "When someone they brought in does something" | **Publish Event Data** on the other person's business event. |
| "Once" | **Reward Behavior** **First Action**, the default. It keys the reward to the person, so each person earns it once at most, and a reward for what another person does is earned once for each such person. |
| "More than once", such as "once every six months" or "each time" | **Reward Behavior** **Every Action**, so that each event can earn it, and **Reward Limit** with **Maximum Count** and **Recent Activity Window** to cap it. A campaign with more than one reward sets **Applicable Reward Name** on each limit, so that each limit counts its own reward. |
| "No more than \$2,000 a year" | **Annual Reward Value Limit**, with the month and day the year starts. |
| "Once for each order or group" | **Reward Behavior** set to **Every Unique Action**, with **Unique Reward Data Parameter** set to the field that names the order or group. |
| "After a waiting period" | **Pending Period** on the reward. Leave **Pending Period Start** blank, so the period starts at the event that earns the reward. Extole runs the reward's rules again when the period ends, so put a condition on how the reward was earned, such as a window from an earlier action, as a trigger rule on the business event that earns it. |
| "Only for some people" | A targeting scenario on the journey, or **Member of Audience** on the reward. |

A rule that compares or adds values reads the business event's value: the data item whose key type
is `VALUE`, such as the order amount. [Unique Event Keys](/technical/troubleshooting/duplicate-action-alerts-and-unique-event-keys#unique-event-keys)
describes key types.

A window is a duration, such as 30 days. On **Has Taken Action** it counts back from the time of
the event being evaluated, and it counts every matching event on the profile dated after the
window's start, including one that arrived earlier with a later date. **Reward Limit** counts back from the moment the reward is evaluated. A
window takes days or hours, not months. For a duration of months, such as "within three months",
use the days of the longest run of that many months: 92 days for "at least three months", so that
no early case passes, and 184 days for "no more than six months", so that no on-time case fails. A
year can start on a date you set: **Annual Reward Value Limit** has a tax year start, and
**Reward Limit** has a tax year starting month. A calendar month is a fixed period, not a
duration, and no window or limit starts on the first of each month. Count it with an expression, as
[Count by Calendar Month](#count-by-calendar-month) describes.

**Has Taken Action** counts every matching event on the profile in the rule's scope. It counts
events, not distinct values, so give each counted event a unique event key when one record must
count once.

A rule sees only the events Extole has recorded. A person whose activity came before your program has
no earlier events on the profile, so a rule that needs earlier activity, such as "has bought
before", fails for them, and a rule that needs a quiet period, such as "no purchase in the last 45
days", passes. A returning customer then looks new: a rule that leaves out a person's first purchase
also leaves out a returning customer whose earlier purchases came before your program. To count that
history, send the past events with their `event_time`, as
[Recent Customer Purchase Upload](/technical/operational-tasks/account-configuration/recent-customer-purchase-upload)
describes.

When a condition fits no row, see whether two business events make it fit. "Active for 30 days"
becomes the event that starts the clock and the event that shows the person is still there,
joined by **Has Taken Action** with a **Minimum Action Age**.

## Count by Calendar Month

Some programs count by calendar month: "six sessions and four homework sets in the same calendar
month", "three months in a row", or "for each child, up to two a month". The month starts on the
first, so an event on 31 October and an event on 1 November count in different months. A **Recent
Activity Window** counts back from the event being evaluated, so it mixes the end of one month with
the start of the next. Put a calendar month on an **Expression Reward Rule** instead. Its expression
reads the business events on the person's profile and sorts them by the month of their date.

1. Add a business event for each counted action, such as `session_attended` and
   `homework_submitted`. Your systems send one event each time the person acts, dated with
   `event_time`.
2. Earn the reward on each counted business event, so its rules run on each action.
3. On the reward, add an **Expression Reward Rule**, and set its expression to the sample below,
   with your own business event names and counts.
4. Remove any **Has Taken Action** and **Reward Limit** that count the same actions or rewards with
   a window. The expression already holds the month.

The expression passes on the event that completes the counts for its calendar month, and on no
other event, so the person earns the reward once in each month. Set the constants at the top for
each condition:

| Constant | What it sets |
|---|---|
| `COUNTS` | Each counted business event, and how many of it a month needs. |
| `MONTHS_IN_A_ROW` | 1 to reward each month. 3 to reward a third month in a row: the expression then passes only when the counts were also met in each of the two months before. A run of six months earns again at the sixth. |
| `GROUP_KEY` | `null` to count all of the person's events together. A data item, such as `student_id`, to count the events of each value apart, so one person earns for each child. |
| `GROUPS_PER_MONTH` | `null` for no limit. A number, such as 2, to pass for no more than that many values of `GROUP_KEY` in one month. |
| `UTC_OFFSET_HOURS` | 0 to read each date's month in UTC. Your time zone's offset, such as -5, to read it there. The offset is fixed, so it does not follow daylight saving time. |

```javascript
javascript@runtime:(function () {
    var COUNTS = { session_attended: 6, homework_submitted: 4 };
    var MONTHS_IN_A_ROW = 1;
    var GROUP_KEY = null;
    var GROUPS_PER_MONTH = null;
    var UTC_OFFSET_HOURS = 0;

    function monthOf(date) {
        var time = new Date(String(date).replace(/\[.*\]$/, '')).getTime();
        var local = new Date(time + UTC_OFFSET_HOURS * 60 * 60 * 1000);
        return local.getUTCFullYear() * 12 + local.getUTCMonth();
    }

    var thisEvent = String(context.getCauseEvent().getRootEventId());
    var container = String(context.getSandbox().getContainer());
    var all = {};
    var before = {};
    var groups = {};
    var thisGroup = null;
    var thisMonth = null;

    function add(tally, group, month, name) {
        var key = group + '|' + month;
        tally[key] = tally[key] || {};
        tally[key][name] = (tally[key][name] || 0) + 1;
    }

    function met(tally, group, month) {
        var counted = tally[group + '|' + month] || {};
        for (var name in COUNTS) {
            if ((counted[name] || 0) < COUNTS[name]) { return false; }
        }
        return true;
    }

    Java.from(context.getPerson().getSteps()).forEach(function (step) {
        var name = String(step.getStepName());
        if (!COUNTS.hasOwnProperty(name)) { return; }
        if (String(step.getCampaignId()) !== String(context.getCampaignId())) { return; }
        if (String(step.getContainer()) !== container) { return; }
        var group = GROUP_KEY === null ? '' : String(step.getData().get(GROUP_KEY));
        var month = monthOf(step.getEventDate());
        groups[group] = true;
        add(all, group, month, name);
        if (String(step.getRootEventId()) === thisEvent) {
            thisGroup = group;
            thisMonth = month;
        } else {
            add(before, group, month, name);
        }
    });

    if (thisGroup === null) {
        context.log('The event being evaluated is not a counted event.');
        return false;
    }
    if (!met(all, thisGroup, thisMonth) || met(before, thisGroup, thisMonth)) {
        context.log('This event does not complete the counts for its month.');
        return false;
    }
    var run = 1;
    while (met(all, thisGroup, thisMonth - run)) { run = run + 1; }
    if (run % MONTHS_IN_A_ROW !== 0) {
        context.log('This month completes a run of ' + run + ' months.');
        return false;
    }
    if (GROUPS_PER_MONTH !== null) {
        var completed = Object.keys(groups).filter(function (group) {
            return group !== thisGroup && met(all, group, thisMonth);
        }).length;
        if (completed >= GROUPS_PER_MONTH) {
            context.log(completed + ' other values already completed this month.');
            return false;
        }
    }
    context.log('Counts complete for ' + (thisGroup || 'the person') + ' in a run of ' + run + ' months.');
    return true;
})();
```

It reads only the business events of this campaign, in the container of the event being evaluated,
so the events of a test person in the campaign sandbox count only in that sandbox. `context.log`
writes why the rule passed or failed to the reward rule's evaluation.

When one person earns for another, such as a parent for a child, count on the earner's profile:

1. Send the child's events with the child's identifier, such as `student_id`, and the earner's
   email.
2. Add **Publish Event Data** to each counted business event in the child's role, with **Person
   Identified by Identity Key** on the earner's email, as
   [Reward One Person for Another Person's Actions](#reward-one-person-for-another-persons-actions)
   describes.
3. In the earner's role, add a business event for each published event, and earn the reward on
   them. Set `COUNTS` to those business events and `GROUP_KEY` to `student_id`.
4. Add `student_id` as a data item on each counted business event, in both roles, or set **Data
   Capture Policy** to **All Data**. A business event records only the data it captures, and the
   expression reads `student_id` from what each one recorded.

A program such as "the parent earns \$20 for each child who completes a month, up to \$40 a month,
and the child earns a gift card for three months in a row" then has two rewards: the parent's,
with `GROUP_KEY` `student_id` and `GROUPS_PER_MONTH` 2, and the child's, with `MONTHS_IN_A_ROW` 3.

Test each side of the month with back-dated events: a test person whose counts are met inside one
month, one whose counts are met only by adding the last days of the month before, one whose third
month follows a month that missed its counts, and, for a limit, a third group in a month that
already has two.

## Reward One Person for Another Person's Actions

**Has Taken Action** counts the events on the profile of the person the reward is for. When the
reward depends on what other people do, each counted action must reach that person's profile:

1. In the role of the person who acts, add a business event for the moment the action counts. Put
   its conditions on it as trigger rules, so the event is recorded only when they pass.
2. To count each person once, give that business event a unique event key from a field that
   identifies the person's place in the group, such as a membership ID. Extole reads the key from
   the inbound event, so trigger this business event with the input event itself.
3. Add **Publish Event Data** to that business event, and set **Target Role** to the role that
   earns the reward.
4. In the earning role, add a business event that the published event triggers.
5. On the reward, add **Has Taken Action** as a reward rule on the business event from step 4,
   with the count you need.

**Person Identification** on **Publish Event Data** decides who receives the event. It arrives
with **Person Identified by Referral Relationship**, which sends the event to the person who
invited the actor. To send the event to a person named in the event data, replace it:

| Person identification | Who receives the event |
|---|---|
| **Person Identified by Identity Key** | The person with the identity key, such as an email, in the event data. |
| **Person Identified By Alternate Person Key** | The person with the person key, such as a partner user ID, in the event data. |
| **Person Identified by Extole Person ID** | The person with the Extole person ID in the event data. |

[How to Set Up Bonus Rewards on a Specific Referral Count](/guides/flow-campaigns/how-to-set-up-bonus-rewards-on-a-specific-referral-count-2nd-3rd-5th-etc)
shows steps 3 to 5 in Flow Builder.

## Connect Your Systems

Once the business events exist, connect each event your systems send to the business event it
starts. An event reaches a campaign as a **trigger event**, declared on the extension or
integration that carries it, and each business event selects its trigger event in **Triggered
By**. A campaign does not publish while a **Triggered By** it needs is empty.

Choose the route for each event, in this order:

| The event comes from | Route | Declared on |
|---|---|---|
| A platform with a packaged integration, such as Shopify | The integration | The integration, which declares its own trigger events |
| Your backend, server to server | The Event API | The **Event API** extension, under **Integration Events** |
| A tag on your website, or a zone your website requests | The JavaScript SDK | The **JavaScript SDK** extension |
| Your iOS or Android app | The Mobile SDK | The **Mobile SDK** extension, under the app's platform |
| A recurring file | File Integration | The job's **Create Events** in **File Integration** |

[How to Set Up Triggered By](/guides/flow-campaigns/how-to-set-up-triggered-by) covers each
route, and [Integrating with Extole](/technical/integration-overview/integrating-with-extole)
compares them.

- **Declare each event once in the account**, with the exact name your systems send, and reuse
  the declaration in every campaign that listens for it.
- **Publish the extension after you declare an event.** A campaign selects trigger events from the
  published version of an extension.
- **Select the declaration in each business event's Triggered By.** The business event then starts
  on that event alone.
- **Give your developers, for each event,** its name, the business moment it records and which
  occurrences to send, the identifier of the person, the same on every event and channel, the
  fields your rules read, such as the value and the unique event key, and `event_time` when they
  send an event after it took place. A test is the same call with the campaign sandbox in its data,
  as [Test a Campaign Draft Over REST](/technical/platform-integrations/rest-apis/test-a-campaign-draft-over-rest)
  describes. [Send Events from the Partner Platform](/technical/building-partner-integrations/integration-types/partner-events-send-from-platform)
  has the call, with retries and testing.

## Add the Experiences People See

A campaign's experiences are what a person sees: how they learn about the offer, how they invite
someone, where an invited person lands, and the email for each reward. Choose them from the
program and from the systems the client has:

| The program needs | Add | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| An offer shown on the client's website or app | A call-to-action, such as a banner or an overlay | The `promotion_viewed` business event, named after the zone the site or app requests |
| An offer sent by email | A promotion email | The `promotion_viewed` business event |
| An invitation from one person to another | The share experience, the share link, and the share email | The first person's share journey |
| A page for the invited person | The landing experience | The second person's `share_clicked` business event |
| A message for each reward | A reward email | The reward's issuer |

- **Write the program's own words.** A copied experience keeps its source's text, such as a
  referral heading and default reward amounts. Set the heading, the message, and each amount for
  the program, and the experience a click opens.
- **Connect each call-to-action to its zone.** A call-to-action selects the zone's trigger event in
  **Triggered By**, declared on the **JavaScript SDK** or **Mobile SDK** extension like any other
  trigger event. [JavaScript SDK and Mobile SDK Extensions](/technical/platform-integrations/extensions/javascript-sdk-and-mobile-sdk-extensions)
  lists the zones each extension declares.
- **Emails take the sender and company details from the account.** The **My Account** settings
  supply the sender, company name, and links, so a reward email needs only its subject and text.
- **A program with no website or app** can reach people by email alone: a promotion email and a
  reward email for each reward.

[Test a Campaign Draft Over REST](/technical/platform-integrations/rest-apis/test-a-campaign-draft-over-rest#view-draft-pages-and-emails)
covers how to check that each experience renders with its content before you publish.

## Test Each Condition from Both Sides

Before you publish, test the draft with test people:

- For each reward, a test person whose events earn it.
- For each window, count, threshold, and exclusion, a test person whose events fall just outside it
  and earn nothing. Back-date events with `event_time` to put them where the window needs them.

Send the test events to the campaign sandbox in the `production` container,
`campaign-<campaign id>-production`. The draft stays unpublished, and each reward comes from its own
supplier, so the test person holds the reward's value and receives its reward email. In the `test`
container, every reward comes from the account's test reward supplier instead.

[Test a Campaign Draft Over REST](/technical/platform-integrations/rest-apis/test-a-campaign-draft-over-rest)
covers how to send the events and read the result.

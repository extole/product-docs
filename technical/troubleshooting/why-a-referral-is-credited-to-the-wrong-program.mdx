title: "Why a Referral Is Credited to the Wrong Program"

---

# Overview

A customer clicks one program's referral link. The link takes them to that program's landing page,
which is correct — and then the experience on that page belongs to a different program. They are
sent on to the other program's thank-you page, and the referral is credited to the other program.

This looks like a broken link, and the link is fine. It happens because **where a referral link
sends someone and which program greets them when they arrive are two separate decisions**, and only
the first one follows the link.

<Note>
This is the friend-side counterpart of
[Why a CTA Opens a Different Program's Share Experience](/technical/troubleshooting/why-a-cta-opens-a-different-programs-share-experience).
That page is about an advocate being handed the wrong program's share experience. This page is
about a friend who received the right link.
</Note>

## The Two Decisions

1. **The redirect.** A referral link belongs to one program, and the program it belongs to decides
   which landing page it opens. This is why the landing page in the address bar is usually right.
2. **The experience on that landing page.** The page asks Extole for a zone, and Extole chooses
   between every live campaign that publishes that zone name. That choice is made again, from
   scratch, and it does not have to agree with the redirect.

Everything the visitor sees after the redirect — the greeting, the offer, the page they are sent to
after signing up — comes from whichever campaign won the second decision. So does the referral
credit.

## Why the Wrong Program Wins

When a referred visitor arrives carrying a referral, Extole tries to place them in a **friend
journey**. It looks at the programs the advocate is already enrolled in and, for each one, asks
which live campaigns could receive a friend for it.

There are two ways the wrong program comes out of that, and they need different fixes.

### Cause One: The Campaign Cannot Receive Its Own Referrals

A campaign is only in that competition if its friend landing experience belongs to the campaign's
**friend** journey. A campaign can instead have that experience built on its advocate-side journey —
the same journey its share experience and advocate pages sit on. When that happens:

- the campaign is **never** a candidate to receive its own referrals;
- Extole falls through to another program the advocate is enrolled in, and picks a live campaign
  whose friend landing experience *is* on its friend journey;
- that campaign answers, and its journey, its pages and its reporting take the referral.

A friend-journey match outranks ordinary program-label selection, so the correct campaign losing
here is not a matter of labels or rules — it never reached the stage where its label would have been
read.

### Cause Two: The Request Does Not Say Which Program It Is

Once every campaign can receive its own referrals, a second problem can remain. The request the
landing page makes carries the advocate's referral code, but **not the program the referral link
belonged to**. With nothing to identify the program, Extole works through the programs the advocate
is enrolled in and takes the first one that can receive a friend.

That list is ordered by which of the advocate's journeys was written to most recently — often a
difference of a fraction of a second, from activity that has nothing to do with the link the friend
clicked. So:

- an advocate enrolled in only one program is always routed correctly;
- an advocate enrolled in several programs is routed to whichever of them their record happened to
  touch last, and every friend who clicks *any* of that advocate's links gets the same answer;
- two testers clicking the same program's link can therefore see two different results, and each of
  them sees their own result consistently.

This is the cause to suspect when a program misroutes for one person and behaves for another, and
when a clean browser changes nothing for either of them.

<Warning>
Every referral for an affected program goes to another program. Those referrals do not appear in the
affected program's reporting, and any reward that depends on them is evaluated under the other
program's rules rather than its own.
</Warning>

## Telling It Apart from a Share Experience Collision

The two problems produce similar complaints and need different fixes. Two questions separate them.

| | Referral credited to the wrong program | [Share experience collision](/technical/troubleshooting/why-a-cta-opens-a-different-programs-share-experience) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who sees it | The friend, after clicking a referral link | The advocate, on a program page |
| Is the referral link itself correct? | Yes | Often no — the advocate is shown another program's link |
| Does a clean browser session fix it? | No. It fails every time | Frequently yes |

A symptom that is intermittent, or that clears in a fresh browser, is the collision. A symptom that
reproduces on every attempt from a clean session is this one.

## How to Confirm It

1. Open the referral link in a clean browser session and check the landing page in the address bar.
   If that page belongs to the right program, the link and the redirect are working.
2. Complete the signup and note which program's thank-you page you are sent to. That page names the
   campaign that actually answered.
3. Repeat from a second clean session. If the wrong program wins every time, the choice is not being
   made by chance and a retest will not clear it.
4. Compare with a program that behaves correctly. Where several programs share one site and only
   some misroute, the difference is in how those campaigns are built, not in the links.
5. Repeat the whole test with a **second advocate's** link for the same program. If one advocate's
   link misroutes and another's does not, it is cause two. If every advocate's link for that program
   misroutes, it is cause one.

## How to Fix It

Both fixes are on the campaign. Contact your Extole team with the list of programs that misroute.

**For cause one,** the friend landing experience has to belong to the campaign's **friend** journey,
the way the rest of the campaign's friend-facing experiences already do. Your Extole team can
confirm which journey each friend landing experience is built on and correct it.

**For cause two,** the referral link's landing page has to be told which program it is serving. Each
program's referral link is configured by a **Share Link Behavior** on the friend side of the
campaign, and its *Destination for Clicks on Share Links* is the address the link opens. Adding the
program's own label to that address as a query parameter pins every visitor who arrives through that
link to that program, whatever else the advocate is enrolled in:

```
https://www.example.com/your-landing-page?extole_required_labels=your-program-label
```

A program label survives a campaign being rebuilt, which a campaign identifier does not, so prefer
the label.

That single field is enough because of two things the platform already does:

- The redirect carries the destination's own query string through to the landing page, alongside the
  advocate code, share channel and UTM parameters it adds itself.
- On the landing page, any query parameter whose name begins `extole_` is passed to every Extole zone
  on that page with the prefix removed — so `extole_required_labels` reaches the zone request as a
  required label.

*Destination for Clicks on Share Links for Mobile Devices* follows the desktop address unless it has
been given one of its own. Check it, and make the same change there if it has. Publish the campaign
when the change is in; it applies to the next click.

<Note>
Adding the label as a **requirement** is what pins the program. Supplying it as a preference —
`extole_labels` rather than `extole_required_labels` — has no effect here, because the journey match
is made before preferences are read.
</Note>

After the change is published, retest with step 1 above from a clean session for each program, and
retest with at least two different advocates, since cause two can behave for one advocate and fail
for another. Until then, treat those programs' referral counts and referral-based rewards as
understated, and the program that is absorbing the traffic as overstated by the same referrals.

<Note>
A campaign can have more than one friend landing experience — a hosted landing page, and an embedded
one that runs inside a page on your own site. They are configured separately, so one can be correct
while the other is not. If the hosted flow behaves and the embedded flow does not, ask for both to be
checked rather than only the one that failed.
</Note>

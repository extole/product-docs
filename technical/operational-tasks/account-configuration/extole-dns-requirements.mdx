## Setting Up Branded URLs

<Info>
**Note**


All domains on this page are just examples. You will pick the specific subdomain for your implementation during your launch.
</Info>

[//]: # "How do I set up my branded program URLs?"

Set up your domain so that links generated and shared are branded with your identity.

There are two places where branded links are important:

* Promotion Links: These are the links you create to promote your program through outbound promotions via email, social media, and other marketing channels (e.g., `refer.mycompany.com/newsletter`).
* Share Links: These are the links your advocates share with their friends (e.g., `refer.mycompany.com/s/lbarnett`).

The most common option is to use subdomains of your main brand domain. For example, If your company website is at `mycompany.com` then the most common branded domains are:

* Promotion Links: `share.mycompany.com`
* Share Links: `share.mycompany.com/s/`

<Warning>
**Important Note**


Your branded domain must be a domain that points to the Extole servers directly, such as `share.mycompany.com`. It cannot be a path like `www.mycompany.com/share`.
</Warning>

## Create CNAMEs for Your Domains

[//]: # "How do I create CNAMEs for my domains?"

Setting up a branded domain will require your IT organization to create CNAMEs for your domains and generate certificates.

To set up your CNAME, complete the following steps:

1. Get your current Extole Program Domain. You can find this in the [My Extole Tech Center](https://my.extole.com/tech-center). It will be listed in the Program Domains section. Typically your unbranded domain will look like "mycompany.extole.io". If you cannot locate your domain, contact your CSM.
2. Log into your domain registrar's control panel and create a CNAME record from your Branded Program URL to the Extole Program Domain. The UI and terminology may vary depending on your registrar. You will point `refer.mycompany.com` to `mycompany.extole.io`.
3. Once you have created your branded domains, go back into Tech Center and create a New Domain. 

**Example DNS update**:

```text
share.mycompany.com. 86400 IN	CNAME	mycompany.extole.io.
```

<Info>
**Corporate DNS versus Public DNS**


Many companies have a separate DNS server that runs inside the company to help support things like a corporate intranet. If this is the case, the CNAME needs to be applied to both the public DNS and the corporate DNS.

If you find your program works correctly on your phone and over public WiFi, but does not work when you are at the office, then this is probably the case.
</Info>

<Info>
**Replacing a Branded Domain**


If you are moving an existing program to a new branded domain, create the new CNAME and leave the old one in place. Extole forwards the old domain so that links your advocates already shared keep working. See [Migrating to a New Program Domain](/guides/platform-overview/implementing-your-referral-program/technical-items/migrating-to-a-new-program-domain).
</Info>

## Certificates for Your Branded Domain

[//]: # "How do I set up certificates for my branded domains?"

Once your CNAME is set up, Extole will automatically generate a certificate for your domain from Let's Encrypt.  These certificates expire every three months so Extole will the automatically renew the certificate each quarter and keep it active for your campaign.

### Setting Up Your Own SSL Certificate (Optional)

If you'd like to create your own certificate, you may do this by fully creating and uploading your own.

After purchasing an SSL certificate from a Certificate Authority, in most cases they'll send you an email or direct you to a location where you can download your certificate. The email or download location may contain a single SSL certificate or multiple SSL certificates.

The following information must be provided to Extole:

* Private Key (as PKCS#8)
* Public Certificate
* Intermediary Chain

To upload your information, go to the My Extole [Tech Center](https://my.extole.com/tech-center) and click on Program Domains. Here you will be able to paste in the files required for your certificate. Tap Save to publish the files.

If your private key is provided in a different format, you may use OpenSSL to convert your private key into PKCS#8.  Please refer to the [OpenSSL Documentation].([https://www.openssl.org/docs/apps/pkcs8.html](https://www.openssl.org/docs/apps/pkcs8.html)) 

It is your responsibility to maintain and renew your certificate. If your certificate in Extole is ever allowed to expire, Extole will automatically generate a new certificate using Let's Encrypt Certificate Authority.

<Info>
**Certificate Signing Request (CSR)**


If you would like to use a Certificate Signing Request please email our Support Team at [support@extole.com](mailto:support@extole.com) and ask us to provide a CSR for your branded domain.
</Info>

## Adding Extole to your SPF Records

[//]: # "How do I add Extole to my SPF records?"

A referral program sends two types of emails:

* Referral Emails: Messages sent from the advocate's email address to the friend's email address.
* Program Emails: Welcome email, reward email, stats emails, etc. are sent from your program to the advocate.  These will typically be sent from `do-not-reply@mycompany.com` using your branded URL.

In the My Extole [Tech Center](https://my.extole.com/tech-center) you will see an **Email Domains** section where you can add your email domain. Here, we display if emails will send from that domain or if there is an SPF/DMARC error to be corrected:

</Frame>

To ensure your emails are delivered, you can add the Extole mail server IP address to your SPF records. You can add `spf.extole.io`.

**Example SPF record update**:

```text Full
mycompany.com.		86400	IN	TXT	"v=spf1 include:spf.extole.io -all"
```

### Adding DKIM Keys

[//]: # "How do I get the DKIM records for my sending domain?"

DKIM lets Extole sign program emails with your own domain so that receiving mail servers accept them. Each sending domain needs three CNAME records, and the host names are specific to that domain.

To get the records, go to the My Extole [Tech Center](https://my.extole.com/tech-center), select **Domains**, and open your domain under **Email Domains**. Click **Generate DKIM records** to produce the exact host and value pairs, then give those pairs to your DNS administrator. Once they resolve, Extole validates and approves them, and the domain's **DKIM Status** changes to Pass.

The domain's **CNAME records** table lists the same pairs at any time, so you can copy them again without regenerating. The **Generate DKIM records** control appears while a domain has no DKIM records yet, or while one of them is failing.

**Example DNS update**:

```text
emXXXX.CompanyName.com          86400 IN CNAME CompanyName.em.extole.io
xtl._domainkey.CompanyName.com  86400 IN CNAME CompanyName.xtl.extole.io
xtl2._domainkey.CompanyName.com 86400 IN CNAME CompanyName.xtl2.extole.io
```

<Warning>
**Use the records Extole generated, not the example**


The lines above show the shape of the records. The host prefix differs for every domain, so publish the values from your own domain's page.
</Warning>

<Warning>
**Program Sending Domain**


If you do not add the records and your SPF or DMARC is set to reject mails from unknown providers, your program emails will be sent from `email.referral-mail.com`.
</Warning>

## Checking an Email Domain

[//]: # "How do I check whether my program emails will send from my own domain?"

Go to the My Extole [Tech Center](https://my.extole.com/tech-center) and select **Domains**. **Email Domains** holds the domains your program emails send from; **Program Domains**, on the same page, holds the branded link domains covered earlier on this page. The two are configured separately.

The **Email Domains** table carries one column per DNS check — **SPF Status**, **DMARC Status**, **DKIM Status**, **MX Status**, and **A Status** — plus **Overall send status**. Select a domain to open its page, which repeats each check with the record Extole found and lists the DKIM **CNAME records** to publish.

**Overall send status** answers the question that decides delivery:

| Status | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Available** | The domain passes validation. Program emails send from an address on this domain. |
| **Not allowed** | The domain fails validation. Program emails send from `email.referral-mail.com` instead. |
| **Overridden** | **Force Send From Email Domain** is on for this domain, so the checks are bypassed. See below. |
| **Checking** | Extole is still reading your DNS. |

Validation requires a passing DMARC record and either a resolving A record or a resolving MX record. **SPF Status** is reported alongside them and does not by itself change the overall status, but a failing SPF record still costs you deliverability — add `spf.extole.io` to your existing SPF record rather than publishing a second one.

<Info>
**Adding a domain to the list**


Use **+ New Domain** to add a sending domain. If you do not see that control, ask your CSM or [Support](mailto:support@extole.com) to add the domain for you; you can generate its DKIM records and check its status yourself afterwards.
</Info>

## Force Send From Email Domain

[//]: # "What does Force Send From Email Domain mean?"

Every email domain carries a **Force Send From Email Domain** switch, shown on the **+ New Domain** form when the domain is created and on the edit form afterwards. The domain's own page shows its state as **Force send**.

With the switch off, Extole checks the From domain on every send and rewrites the From address to `email.referral-mail.com` when that domain does not pass. With it on, Extole skips those checks for this domain and sends from the address you configured, whatever the DNS checks report. The domain's validation then reports as passing and its overall send status reads **Overridden**.

Turn it on only when a seed test has confirmed that mail from the domain reaches the inbox and Extole's automatic checks reject a configuration you know works.

<Warning>
**An override changes Extole, not the receiving mail server**


Forcing the send domain bypasses Extole's checks. It does not change how receiving mail servers treat your messages, and some providers may consider mail from an unverified domain invalid and quarantine or reject it.
</Warning>

<Info>
**It is not a step in DKIM setup**


Adding a domain, publishing its DKIM records, and validating it are separate from this switch. Leave it off for a normal setup and fix the underlying DNS records instead.
</Info>

For what Extole does when a domain is not verified, including the From address advocates' friends see on share emails, see [Why Is the From Address @email.referral-mail.com?](/guides/notifications-and-troubleshooting/platform-and-admin/why-is-the-from-email-email-referral-mail-com-gmail-friendly-from-changes).

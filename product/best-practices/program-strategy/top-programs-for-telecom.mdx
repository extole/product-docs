## Telecom's Real Acquisition Channel Isn't Paid Media — It's the People Already Touching the Customer

Few industries spend more per new customer than telecom. Markets are saturated, switching offers from competitors are constant, and paid acquisition costs keep climbing while converting worse than word of mouth ever does. Meanwhile, an MSO (multiple system operator) or telecom provider has something most brands don't: an army of people already standing in front of the customer at exactly the right moment — a technician finishing an install, a leasing agent showing an apartment, a channel partner closing a business deal.

A consumer referral program captures a slice of that. It's the right place to start, but it only reaches customers who already have an existing customer to refer them — it does nothing for the property manager who controls whether every unit in a new building gets your service or a competitor's, the field technician standing in a customer's living room, or the B2B channel partner who could hand you an entire office building's contract in one deal.

The progression below — Refer a Friend, then Employee/Field Ambassador, then MDU (multi-dwelling unit)/Property Manager, then B2B — adds one of those channels at a time, each building on the infrastructure of the one before it. Run all four, with Offer programs layered in between, and you're not running a referral program anymore — you're running an acquisition engine with your own workforce, your real estate partners, and your enterprise sales motion all pointed at the same goal.

## Stage 1: Consumer Refer a Friend — The Foundation

Most telecom providers already run some version of [Refer a Friend](/product/product-overview/programs/refer-a-friend/index), and for good reason — it's the fastest way to convert existing subscribers' trust into new orders. The flow tracks the events specific to a telecom customer journey — order placed, service installed, account activated — so rewards are tied to a completed install, not just a signup that might not stick.

Typical [benchmarks for the vertical](/guides/dashboards-and-reporting/extole-dashboards-and-metrics/how-to-measure-and-benchmark-your-referral-program-success) give a useful gut check on health: an order rate (orders divided by signups) around 38%, and a service install rate (installs divided by orders) around 80%. If your program is below those, the gap is more likely in promotion or reward strength rather than in the program's setup.

These are the first things to try:

- **Run a burst or test offer.** A time-limited boost to the reward, such as a holiday or back-to-school offer, gives people a fresh reason to share. Test the advocate offer and the friend offer separately so you can tell which one made the difference.
- **Make it visible in your app and account.** Put an always-on referral link in the app and on the account and billing pages, and use push notifications or a login pop-up to bring it up. Customers can't share a program they never see.
- **Market after the install.** Send a text or email right after a successful install, when the customer is happiest, and have technicians leave a printed card with a QR code behind.
- **Ask at other good moments.** An order confirmation, a bill payment, or a support call that went well are all times when customers feel positive about you.
- **Keep the reward simple and quick.** Say plainly what the advocate and the friend each get, and deliver the reward fast, so people see that the program works and share again.

As with any first-stage program, its value isn't limited to what it earns on its own. The order and install events flowing into Extole here are the same events every stage below reuses — which means each new program is new configuration on data you already have, not a new integration.

## Stage 2: Employee / In-the-Field Ambassador — Turn Every Install Into a Referral Opportunity

A technician standing in a customer's home after a successful install, or a store associate closing a sale, is talking to someone at the single best moment to ask for a referral — happy, engaged, and standing right in front of the person who can make the ask. A pure digital referral program never reaches that moment; an [employee program](/product/product-overview/programs/refer-a-friend/employee-referral-program) is built for exactly it.

This is where **[Go Extole](/product/product-overview/programs/refer-a-friend/go-extole)**, Extole's field team app, matters most. It puts the entire program on an employee's phone: they can pull up a personal QR code or share link on the spot, hand it to the customer during the visit, and see their own referral activity — shares, signups, installs — without waiting for a desktop report days later. No printed flyers, no separate system to check; the moment happens and the tracking happens with it.

Top programs let employees earn uncapped rewards for referring home internet installations — no ceiling on what a single employee can earn keeps the incentive alive well past the first few referrals, which matters for a channel you want people to keep using month after month, not just try once.

Three practices help an employee program stay active:

- **Run a monthly drawing.** Enter employees based on their referral activity, so every referral is another chance to win and a slow month still has a reason to keep going.
- **Show employees their results.** The Go Extole app shows each employee their referrals and how much they have earned, so the program stays in front of them without a separate report.
- **Train the team and get them excited.** Employees need to know how the program works, how to make the ask at the end of a visit, and why it matters. A short training session and visible support from managers make them more likely to use it.

## Stage 3: MDU / Property Manager / Leasing Agent — The Standout Program

If there's one program in this progression to lead with, it's this one. A single property manager or leasing agent doesn't influence one customer — they influence every unit in a building, every lease renewal, every move-in packet handed to a new tenant. Get a property manager promoting your service and you've effectively recruited a distribution channel for an entire building, not a single referral.

The property manager isn't the customer and isn't quite a traditional advocate either — they're the person who asks on your behalf. In practice that means giving them:

- **A personal dashboard** showing their referrals, conversions, and rewards earned — so a property manager who's driving real volume can see it, and so can you.
- **A link and QR code built for in-person moments** — a leasing agent handing over keys, a manager posting it in a mailroom or community board, a mover meeting a new tenant on move-in day. No app to open, no link to type — just a scan.

There is a third person in these programs: the Account Manager who manages the property managers. Account Managers can have their own dashboards to see how each property manager is performing, highlight the top performers, and push referrals across the properties they cover.

This program tends to outperform other programs for a simple reason: a property manager saying "here's the internet provider for this building" carries the weight of an official recommendation, not a personal opinion — and it reaches every future resident of that building, not just the one person in front of them.

## Stage 4: B2B — A Different Flow, a Bigger Prize

Everything above runs on more or less the same shape: someone shares, someone signs up, someone installs, usually within days. B2B doesn't work that way, and the program built for it shouldn't pretend it does. A referred business doesn't convert on a share link — it becomes a lead, gets worked by a sales team, and closes on its own timeline, sometimes weeks or months out.

That means a B2B program plugs into your CRM rather than a checkout flow: a referral creates a lead record, a sales rep works it like any other opportunity, and the outcome — won or lost — gets reported back to Extole so the right person gets credit and rewarded once the deal actually closes. Rewards tend to scale with what's at stake, too — a small business account and an enterprise contract aren't worth the same referral bonus, so tiered rewards based on deal size keep the incentive proportional to the win.

Keep these rewards higher than the other programs since the impact is larger too.

## The Always-On Layer: Offer Programs

All four stages above are acquisition-shaped — new customers, new accounts, new buildings. [Offer/Reward for Action programs](/product/product-overview/programs/offer/index) run alongside them, rewarding existing subscribers for actions that raise their value or make them harder to churn. Three worth having on at all times:

- **Upgrading service.** A subscriber on a base tier is a prime candidate for a higher tier — internet speed, a premium channel package, added mobile lines. Rewarding the upgrade directly turns "maybe someday" into a decision made this week.
- **Adding a bundled service.** A customer with internet only and no TV, phone, or mobile line is an easy expansion target. An Offer program around the specific add-on you want to grow converts that gap without needing a full re-sell of the account.
- **Enabling autopay or paperless billing.** Small ask, real payoff — fewer missed payments, lower servicing cost per account — and subscribers respond well to a modest reward for a one-click change.

Depending on the offer, they may run on the same account and billing events already flowing into Extole for referral, which means they're some of the fastest programs to stand up in this entire progression.

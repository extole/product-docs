## You're Probably Already Ready

If you're only running one referral program today, the honest answer is you're probably ready for a second one already. A single referral program means that you are missing out on key opportunities to increase acquisition, retention, and engagement.

So don't think of this as a checklist for whether to add a program — for most partners, adding one is worth doing regardless. What actually varies is when it makes the most sense to do it. The signals below aren't readiness tests; they're the moments where a second program stops being "something to think about eventually" and becomes an obvious next step — either because something about your first program is telling you it's plateauing, or because something happening in your business just created a reason to launch.

## Signals From Your Business

These come from what's happening on your end. They're often the strongest signals, because you're already thinking about the moment; a second program gives you a way to act on it.

- **Holiday and seasonal moments.** Peak shopping windows, open enrollment, tax season, back-to-school — any period where you're already running promotions is a natural home for a limited-time [Offer program](/product/product-overview/programs/offer/index) or a dedicated MDU (multi-dwelling unit) push layered on top of what's evergreen.
- **A new product or service launch.** Launches need awareness and adoption, fast. An Offer/Reward for Action program built around trying the new thing (or an [Ambassador](/product/product-overview/programs/influencer) push to seed it with your most engaged customers) gets there faster than waiting for organic referral to catch up.
- **New branches or locations opening.** A new location needs local awareness before it has local word-of-mouth. A [Welcome Offer](/product/product-overview/programs/welcome-offer/index) or an in-person/[employee referral](/product/product-overview/programs/refer-a-friend/employee-referral-program) push gives it a running start instead of waiting for your existing program's reach to trickle down.
- **New platform features.** A feature nobody's using is a stalled investment. Rewarding adoption directly — connecting an account, enabling a setting, downloading an app update — is exactly what an Offer program is built for.
- **New partners.** A new partnership usually comes with its own audience you haven't reached yet. These programs allow you to easily track performance and reward these partners separately and can scale immensely.

## Signals From Your First Program

These come from how your existing program is performing, not the calendar. Any one of them is reason enough to look at a second program.

- **You've had tenure on your first program.** Six-plus months into a stable, evergreen program, you've learned its rhythms — your audience, your reward economics, your creative. That comfort is what makes adding a second program manageable instead of overwhelming. In your first 90 days, it may be too early — you're still tuning program one.
- **Participation or conversion has plateaued.** If share rate, conversion rate, or overall volume has flattened out after a period of growth, that's the clearest signal there is. It usually means your program has reached its natural audience — the people who were going to share or convert already have — and no amount of creative refresh or burst campaign fully fixes that, because the constraint isn't the offer, it's that referral is the only program running. A second program reaches people referral was never going to reach.
- **FOMO from different audiences.** If you are getting feedback that people want to participate and are currently not eligible (for example, employees, agents, or partners), this is an opportunity to give them their own offer and incentive to refer.

The through line: none of these need your first program to be underperforming. They're moments where your business is already in motion, and a second program is how you capture that instead of leaving it on the table.

## Matching the Signal to the Program

| Signal | What it usually means | Worth adding |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Holiday/seasonal window | A promotional moment already exists | Time-limited Offer program, or a referral [burst](/product/best-practices/rewarding-best-practices/limited-time-bursts) |
| New product/service launch | Needs fast awareness and adoption | Offer/Reward for Action (trial), or Ambassador (seed with advocates) |
| New branch/location | No local word-of-mouth yet | Welcome Offer, or an employee/in-person referral push |
| New feature | Adoption is stalled | Offer/Reward for Action targeting that specific action |
| New partner | A new audience you haven't reached | A partner-specific program or campaign |
| Stable tenure (6+ months) on program one | Team has capacity to take on more | Whichever job is weakest for you today (see [Why Clients Need More Than Referral](/product/best-practices/program-strategy/why-clients-need-more-than-referral)) |
| Participation/conversion plateau | Referral has saturated its natural audience | Welcome Offer or Offer/Reward for Action — reach people referral can't |
| FOMO from other audiences | People who want to participate (employees, agents, partners) aren't eligible today | A dedicated program or offer for that audience |

This table is a starting point, not a rulebook — several signals often point to the same program, and the right pick still depends on what you already have running.

## What to Do When You Spot One

- **Don't wait for a perfect signal.** If any of the above sound familiar, it's worth a conversation — you don't need every box checked before it's worth exploring a second program.
- **Lead with what's concrete.** "We're opening three new branches next quarter" is a stronger starting point than "our conversion rate has plateaued." Bring whatever specific moment is coming up; your Extole team can help match it to the right program.
- **Overlapping signals make an even stronger case.** A plateaued referral program, a holiday window, and a renewal all landing around the same time isn't three separate things — it's one strong reason to act, seen from three angles.
- **Talk to your CSM.** They can help translate any of these signals into a specific program recommendation and walk you through setup once you're ready to add one. See [Acquisition, Retention, Engagement: Why Clients Need More Than Referral](/product/best-practices/program-strategy/why-clients-need-more-than-referral) for how to think about which job a second program should do for you.

## Extole Is More Than a Referral Platform

Most conversations about Extole start with Refer a Friend, and it's easy to see why — referral is our best-known program and a genuinely great acquisition engine. But referral is one program type within a larger offer management system. Extole is the way to configure, launch, and reward almost any activity that creates value for a business, not just "get a new customer."

That matters because a customer's relationship with a brand doesn't end at signup. It runs through three phases — getting acquired, sticking around, and staying engaged — and each phase has moments where the right offer, delivered at the right time, moves the needle. Referral is one of those moments. Welcome offers, ambassador programs, reward-for-action offers, and loyalty integrations are others.

The reason to think in terms of "programs," plural, instead of just "the referral program," is that a client running one program is capturing one slice of the value Extole can drive across that relationship. Clients running several programs together, timed to different moments in the lifecycle, tend to be our stickiest and best-performing accounts.

## Every Program Does One of Three Jobs

Strip away the branding and every Extole program is doing one of three things for the business: bringing in new customers, keeping existing ones, or getting more out of the relationship day to day. Mapping our program types to those jobs makes it obvious why a client shouldn't stop at one.

### Acquisition — Bringing New Customers In

- [Refer a Friend](/product/product-overview/programs/refer-a-friend/index): existing customers vouch for the brand and bring friends in the door.
- [Welcome Offer](/product/product-overview/programs/welcome-offer/index): a new visitor is offered a reason to convert on their very first visit, before they've decided to become a customer at all.
- [Employee referral programs](/product/product-overview/programs/refer-a-friend/employee-referral-program): staff act as an acquisition channel using their own networks.
- MDU (multi-dwelling unit) and partner programs: enable existing partnerships to drive new customers.

### Retention — Keeping the Customers Already Acquired

- Loyalty integrations: points, tiers, and member status give customers a reason to stay rather than shop around.
- [Ambassador programs](/product/product-overview/programs/influencer): a smaller, more invested group of advocates gets an ongoing relationship with the brand (a portal, recognition, better rewards), which correlates strongly with staying longer.
- Welcome Offer also sets up retention: a cross-sell welcome offer can prompt your customers to add on additional products, upsell to a paid membership, and more.

### Engagement — Getting More From Customers Already There

- [Offer / Reward for Action programs](/product/product-overview/programs/offer/index): reward a specific behavior that correlates with a healthy, high-value customer — downloading the app, connecting an account, leaving a review, trying a new product line.
- Audience-based referral, again: give a top-tier customer a better offer than the average customer. This strengthens their loyalty (the "flywheel effect" — customers who refer are measurably more likely to refer again).

Notice that referral shows up in more than one row. That's the point: no program is purely one job. But most clients only ever activate referral's acquisition role and leave its retention and engagement upside on the table — and never touch the program types built specifically for those other two jobs.

## Why Running Programs Together Compounds Value, Not Just Adds It

It's tempting to think of a second or third program as incremental — a bit more revenue on top of what referral already drives. In practice, layered programs reinforce each other in ways that a single program can't:

- **They cover more of the lifecycle at once.** A customer who isn't a candidate for referral this month (too new, hasn't had a great experience yet) may be exactly the right candidate for a Welcome Offer or an engagement-driving Offer program. Running only referral means missing everyone who isn't in a shareable moment right now.
- **Each program feeds the others.** A Welcome Offer that gets someone through a strong first experience makes them a better referral candidate later. An Offer program that rewards app adoption produces a more engaged customer who is statistically more likely to advocate. Retention and engagement work isn't separate from acquisition — it's what makes the next round of acquisition cheaper and higher quality.
- **Advocacy itself compounds.** This is the flywheel effect: customers acquired through referral tend to refer more themselves, as much as four to five times more often than customers acquired another way. A program that turns customers into repeat advocates (ambassador, loyalty) is quietly making every other acquisition program more efficient over time.
- **No single point of failure.** A client running only referral has all of their program performance tied to one lever — one creative, one incentive, one moment in the journey. A client running three or four programs has multiple, independent ways to hit a growth number, so a soft quarter for one program doesn't sink the whole strategy.

## Example: A Credit Union Layering Three Programs

Say a credit union asks, "What can I be doing to grow my program?" The reflexive answer is to optimize Refer a Friend — better creative, a bigger incentive, a new placement. All useful, but it only pulls on the acquisition lever.

A lifecycle answer looks more like this:

| Program | Job | What it looks like for the credit union |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| [Welcome Offer for Credit Unions](/product/product-overview/programs/welcome-offer/welcome-offer-for-credit-unions) | Acquisition | A new member gets a reward for joining and completing a qualifying action, with no promo code required — first touch is frictionless. |
| Refer a Friend | Acquisition + engagement | Existing members refer people in their network; sharing itself is one of the highest-engagement actions a member can take. |
| Offer / Reward for Action | Engagement + retention | Members who already joined are rewarded for signing up for e-statements, setting up direct deposit, or downloading the app — the actions most correlated with a member staying long-term. |

Run together, these three programs mean the credit union has a way to reach a member at essentially every stage: the day they join, the moment they're happy enough to tell a friend, and the months after when the goal shifts from "acquire" to "deepen the relationship" using rewards as an incentive. That's a materially different conversation than "how do we get more referrals," and the same logic applies to any industry — retail, telecom, subscription — by swapping in the program types that fit that vertical's best practices. ## What This Means in Practice

- **Lead with the lifecycle, not the program.** When a client asks how to grow, the first question is which of the three jobs is underserved — acquisition, retention, or engagement — not which referral tactic to tweak next.
- **Recommend combinations, not single programs.** A client running only Refer a Friend is a client with two open jobs. The strongest recommendations pair an acquisition program with at least one retention or engagement program suited to their industry.

## Your Customers Are Already Your Best Marketing Channel

Retail shoppers don't just buy — they gift, recommend, post, and talk about the brands they love, often to exactly the people most likely to shop with you next. Paid media tries to reach those people from the outside; the programs below let your customers do it from the inside, with credibility an ad can't buy.

Most retail brands start with a single Refer a Friend program, and it's a great start. But a referral program only captures one kind of sharing: "you should try this brand." Your customers do plenty of other kinds — "I want this specific thing for my birthday," "here's what I'm wearing," "here's a code for my family," "I've been loyal to this brand for years." Each of those is a different moment, and each can have a program built for it.

The progression below — [Refer a Friend](/product/product-overview/programs/refer-a-friend/index), then [Drop a Hint](/product/product-overview/programs/drop-a-hint), then [Ambassador and Influencer](/product/product-overview/programs/influencer) programs, with [Friends & Family](/product/product-overview/programs/friends-family) and [Loyalty](/product/product-overview/programs/loyalty/index) rounding it out — adds one of those moments at a time. Each one runs on the same platform and the same customer data, so every program you add is mostly configuration, not a new project.

## Stage 1: Refer a Friend — The Foundation

If you're a retailer, there's a good chance you already run this one. Refer a Friend turns a purchase into an invitation: a happy customer shares your brand, a friend gets a reason to try it, and both are rewarded when the friend buys. It works across online and in-store, and it's the program every other stage builds on.

A useful gut check is how you compare to typical retail results, which you can track with the [referral program benchmarks](/guides/dashboards-and-reporting/extole-dashboards-and-metrics/how-to-measure-and-benchmark-your-referral-program-success). General retail programs see around a 7.7% share rate, a 16.2% signup rate, and a 10.8% conversion rate; apparel and accessories brands typically see conversion closer to 15.8%. If you're well below those, the gap is usually in promotion and [reward strength](/product/best-practices/rewarding-best-practices/referral-reward-strategy-retail), not the program's setup.

The real payoff of getting this stage right is what it sets up. The purchase and share events flowing into Extole for referral (see [referral program business events](/product/product-overview/events/referral-events)) are the same events Drop a Hint, Influencer, Friends & Family, and Loyalty programs all use, so each program you add after this is new configuration on data you already have, not a new integration.

## Stage 2: Drop a Hint — "Perfect for You" Becomes "Perfect for Me"

If Refer a Friend says "you should try this brand," Drop a Hint says "here's exactly what I want." A customer picks a product, chooses their size and color, adds a personal note, and sends it to the people most likely to buy it for them — by email, text, WhatsApp, or any one-to-one channel. The recipient gets the product details and a direct link to buy.

It's a natural next program because it needs no reward budget. Most Drop a Hint programs run without any incentive at all, since the gift itself is the motivation. That makes it one of the lowest-cost programs in your lineup, and it tends to convert better than regular site traffic. It can also lower returns, because people are buying things the recipient has already said they want.

It's especially strong in a few moments:

- **New product launches.** Customers share the new item with their networks, either to spread the word or as a gift idea once it ships.
- **Holiday wishlists.** Customers tell friends and family exactly which item, size, and color they want, without building a registry.
- **Big-ticket items.** People rarely buy expensive products on a whim, but they'll happily ask someone else to.

A bonus: the [Shared Products report](/product/product-overview/programs/drop-a-hint#performance-and-data) shows you every product customers share. That's a read on what people want but aren't buying yet, which you can use to build promotions around those items. The [Drop a Hint asset guide](/guides/programs-and-campaigns/asset-guides/drop-a-hint-asset-guide) covers the share experience and emails.

## Stage 3: Ambassador / Influencer — Treat Your Best Advocates Differently

In most referral programs, [about 20% of customers drive 80% of the sharing](/guides/programs-and-campaigns/other-acquisition-and-engagement-programs/run-targeted-super-advocate-programs). An Ambassador program is how you recognize that group: give them a personal share link or code, a dashboard that shows what they've earned, and rewards that are richer than the ones in your everyday referral program. For example, an ambassador might earn 20% off where a regular advocate earns 15%, with a bonus once the people they refer have spent a set amount.

Two things make it easy to start. First, it needs no technical work beyond what's already in place for Refer a Friend. Second, ambassadors don't have to go through your website's share flow. They get a welcome email with their personal link and can drop it straight into an Instagram bio, a newsletter, or a text thread.

Your "ambassadors" don't have to be social media influencers. They can be super advocates, VIP customers, partners, affiliates, or professionals who recommend your product. Two patterns show the range:

- **A fitness instructor program.** An activewear brand invited fitness instructors to be ambassadors, giving them an easy way to share with their classes and clients. With performance tracking and custom rewards, it became one of that brand's primary revenue drivers.
- **A creator program that pays on sales.** A beauty brand could invite creators and its most loyal customers into an ambassador program. Each one gets a personal link and earns 5% of the cart value of every purchase made through it, with payouts sent through [PayPal](/guides/rewards-management/sending-rewards-with-tremendous). Because the reward follows what friends actually spend, bigger orders earn more, and the brand's cost stays a fixed share of the sales it brings in.

When you launch, you choose [how people join](/guides/audiences-and-segmentation/segmentation/converting-advocates-to-influencers): no application (you invite people you already know), auto approval (applicants are accepted or declined by rules you set, such as purchase history or loyalty tier), or manual approval (your team reviews each applicant). Pick the one that matches how you'll run the program on day one, since it's not easy to switch later.

## Stage 4: Friends & Family — An Exclusive Offer Your Best Customers Can Share

A Friends & Family program gives a VIP audience, such as your top customers, advocates, or employees, an exclusive offer and the ability to share it with a small circle. It works like an invitation-only sale: a richer discount than your everyday referral reward, and a cap on how many people each member can send it to, so the offer stays special and doesn't dilute your brand.

There are two versions. Standard Friends & Family gives your VIPs something like an early-access discount to use themselves and share with three to five friends. Give a Gift lets members send a highly exclusive reward, such as a free month of a membership, to a limited number of friends, usually with no reward for the sender.

Because the offers are bigger and the audience is already loyal, these programs often deliver higher share, conversion, and acquisition rates than standard Refer a Friend. They also fit a lot of retail moments: early access to a sale or collection, a limited-time offer, a boost during a slow season, an employee discount program, or a surprise-and-delight thank you.

Two patterns show the range:

- **An always-on VIP tier.** A global athletic brand offers an exclusive deal to its top-tier members and their friends. Because it's always on, new members are folded in automatically as they reach the tier.
- **A global employee and partner program.** A consumer electronics brand opened a program to employees and partners worldwide, with language and rewards localized by country: a 50% employee discount shareable with up to eight friends, plus a standing 20% discount for those friends and partners. Reporting let the brand track performance by department and employee.

It's easy to run: promote it by email to the audience you've chosen, send an announcement, a reminder, and a last-chance email, and keep the offer behind a login so it stays exclusive. The [Friends & Family asset guide](/guides/programs-and-campaigns/asset-guides/friends-family-asset-guide) covers the emails.

## Stage 5: Loyalty — Reward the Whole Relationship, Not Just One Purchase

Every program so far rewards a single outcome: a referral, a hint, a sale. A loyalty program rewards what customers do over time. Members earn points for the actions you choose, collect them in a [Reward Bank](/technical/platform-integrations/extensions/reward-bank), and redeem them in a branded [Redemption Center](/product/product-overview/programs/loyalty/member-experience), so there's always a reason to come back.

It works in three parts:

- **[Ways to earn](/product/product-overview/programs/loyalty/ways-to-earn).** Each is a customer action paired with a reward rule: a purchase, a review, a profile completion, or a referral. You decide what earns points and how many.
- **Accumulation.** Points build up in the member's balance and persist across campaigns and channels, so the member decides when to spend them.
- **[Redemption](/product/product-overview/programs/loyalty/redemption-and-rewards).** Members trade points for the rewards you offer, such as a percentage-off code, a gift with purchase, a gift card, or a charitable donation. You set the conversion rate, and you can skip the redemption step entirely for rewards you'd rather issue automatically.

This is where the earlier programs start to reinforce each other. A referral can earn points, so sharing becomes one more way to build a balance. A loyalty program can also be the goal of a referral, with advocates inviting friends to join and both getting a welcome reward. Because a balance grows over time, the best placements are recurring ones: a balance on the account page, a nudge when a member is close to a reward, and a confirmation every time points are earned.

It runs on the same rules engine as every other Extole program, so the quality checks, reward limits, and reporting you already use apply here too. To get started, see [How to Set Up a Loyalty Program](/guides/programs-and-campaigns/how-to-set-up-a-loyalty-program).

## The Full Picture

| Stage | Program | The moment it captures | Why it matters |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 1 | [Refer a Friend](/product/product-overview/programs/refer-a-friend/index) | "You should try this brand" | The foundation; every other program reuses its data |
| 2 | [Drop a Hint](/product/product-overview/programs/drop-a-hint) | "This is exactly what I want" | Usually no reward cost; converts above typical site traffic |
| 3 | [Ambassador / Influencer](/product/product-overview/programs/influencer) | Your most influential fans sharing at scale | Personal links, a dashboard, and richer rewards for the 20% who drive most sharing |
| 4 | [Friends & Family](/product/product-overview/programs/friends-family) | A VIP offer shared with a small circle | Bigger offers lift share and conversion rates; capped sharing protects the brand |
| 5 | [Loyalty](/product/product-overview/programs/loyalty/index) | Ongoing engagement over time | Points keep customers coming back, and referrals can earn them |

Each program adds a moment without touching the ones already running, and all of them run on the platform and data you already have. If you're only on Stage 1 today, the next conversation with your CSM is about which stage to add next. Drop a Hint is often the quickest to launch, and Ambassador is where your best customers will notice the difference. For why layering programs works in any industry, see [Acquisition, Retention, Engagement: Why Clients Need More Than Referral](/product/best-practices/program-strategy/why-clients-need-more-than-referral).

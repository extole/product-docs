## Executive Summary

Contribution rate is the share of all new customers that come from your Extole programs. The clients that reach a 10% contribution rate, or one in every ten new customers coming from Extole, don't get there with one refer-a-friend page. They run a portfolio: several advocate audiences, an always-on offer boosted by a planned calendar of promotions, and referral placed inside the moments customers already use.

Across the Extole programs reviewed for this playbook, the leaders share three things:

- **More advocates than just customers.** Employees, ambassadors, business customers, partners, super advocates and affinity groups each get their own program, offer and messaging.
- **A promotional rhythm, not a static offer.** A steady evergreen offer is lifted by quarterly and seasonal bursts, each one run as a controlled test so the next one is smarter. Some programs do this as often as once a week.
- **Referral built into the product and the customer journey.** In-app and logged-in experiences, triggered emails at high-satisfaction moments, and reminders that bring advocates back.

This playbook describes what those programs do and the strategies any brand can adopt to get there.

## What Contribution Rate Means, and Why 10%

Contribution rate is the share of all new customers in a period that come from referral and advocacy programs. If a brand acquires 10,000 new customers in a quarter and 1,000 of them came through a referral, ambassador, employee or partner link, its contribution rate is 10%. You can track yours with the [New Customer Contribution Rate report](/guides/dashboards-and-reporting/new-customer-contribution-rate-report).

It is the clearest single measure of whether referral is a real acquisition channel or a side program. Share counts and conversion rates show how well a program performs; contribution rate shows how much it matters to the business.

Why aim for 10%:

- **It makes referral a material channel.** At one in ten new customers, referral earns a place alongside paid search and social in planning and budget conversations.
- **Referred customers are worth more.** A commissioned Forrester Total Economic Impact study of Extole customers measured a 150% increase in average order value for referred customers.
- **It compounds.** Referred customers are up to 5x more likely to use a referral program themselves, so every referred customer adds future advocates.
- **It is within reach.** Extole reports that well-run programs can drive up to 25% of new customers. Ten percent is a stretch for most brands, but the leaders show it can be done.

Programs that rely on a single evergreen page rarely get there. The rest of this playbook shows how the leaders do.

## What Best in Class Looks Like

Eleven practices show up again and again in the strongest programs. None is exotic; the difference is that leaders do them frequently.

| Practice | Examples of what it looks like |
| :---- | :---- |
| Promote it constantly, everywhere | Referral appears in every owned channel, every month: email, the app, statements and bills, order or account confirmations, the website, social, and in-person and agent conversations. It is marketed like a product, not launched once and left on a page. |
| Pull referral data into their own reporting | Referral results flow into the BI, CRM and marketing systems. Contribution is reported alongside paid and other channels, and the data is used to target advocates, time offers and decide what to test next. |
| Multiple advocate audiences | Separate programs for customers, employees, business customers, partners, creators and ambassadors. |
| Segment-specific offers | Tailored offers and creative by product or line of business, life stage or affinity group, such as a checking account vs. a mortgage, internet vs. mobile, or a monthly vs. annual plan. |
| Evergreen plus bursts | Always-on offer, lifted by quarterly and seasonal promotions timed to each category's demand peaks, such as back to school, moving season, tax time, the holidays or a product launch. |
| Test-and-learn on every burst | Control vs. treatment on both the advocate and friend offer. |
| Gamified advocate rewards | Milestone bonuses, tiers, rewards for sharing, sweepstakes entries, leaderboards. |
| Embedded, logged-in experiences | Referral inside the app, online banking or account portal and on confirmation pages, not only a landing page. |
| Lifecycle and reactivation | Triggered asks at happy moments, such as after an install, a first purchase, a funded account or a high satisfaction score; win-back offers for lapsed advocates. |
| Reward for action and offer programs | Rewards for steps that make a customer more valuable, such as downloading the app, setting up direct deposit, adding a product, writing a review or joining loyalty. |
| Offline and assisted channels | Referral captured by branch staff, call-center agents, store associates and field teams. |

## Client Spotlights

### Fortune 500 Telecom: A Network of Advocate Programs

This provider treats referral as a network of advocate programs rather than a single campaign. Residential customers, business (SMB) customers and employees on both the consumer and business sides each have their own program. On top of that sit partner and ambassador programs for leasing agents, realtors, preferred partners, community organizations and event teams, plus three-party referral programs with enterprise and SMB partners.

- **Property channel.** Leasing agents get personalized links and rewards, with a dedicated manager dashboard for multi-dwelling units.
- **Quarterly bursts.** Customer and employee programs are lifted at the end of each quarter, in September and over the holidays.
- **Top-of-funnel capture.** A lead-capture offer on retail pages turns browsers into prospects the referral program can then convert.
- **Fulfillment-aware attribution.** An eligibility window credits referrals even when the order and install events arrive out of sequence.

### High-AOV Financing Retailer: The Test-and-Learn Engine

This brand runs a steady cadence of themed promotions, from spring getaways to holiday shopping sprees, and treats each one as an experiment.

- **2x2 test design.** Advocate offer and friend offer are tested independently (control and test on each side), so every campaign reveals which lever moved results.
- **Milestone rewards.** Advocates earn more as they refer more, with bonuses at set referral counts.
- **Sweepstakes layered on the base offer.** Entries per share and per successful referral create urgency without raising the per-referral cost.
- **Win-back for dormant advocates.** A richer offer targets advocates who have gone quiet.
- **Affinity programs.** Bonus offers and nomination programs for groups such as federal employees, unions, healthcare and education workers.

### Major Credit Card Issuer: Segmentation and Compliance at Scale

This issuer runs a separate referral experience for each card product and audience. Consumer and student cards each have their own program, and a parent-to-student program reaches families at back-to-school time.

- **Product-specific creative.** Each card's benefits carry through the share message, landing page and emails.
- **Offline referral path.** Dedicated programs capture referrals that start outside digital channels.
- **Share reminders and SMS testing.** Reminder emails re-prompt advocates, and SMS share copy is A/B tested.
- **Servicing-style notifications.** Earned-reward and share emails are sent as account servicing messages, so they reliably reach the customer.
- **Disciplined refresh cycle.** Claims and creative are updated on a regular schedule without disrupting the live program.

### Major Online Bank: One Brand, Many Products

This bank runs distinct referral programs for checking and savings, small business banking, mortgage and personal lending.

- **Embedded experiences.** The advocate and friend experiences live inside online and mobile banking.
- **Holiday burst.** A seasonal lift on the double-sided offer, for both consumer and small business.
- **Abandonment offers.** A separate offer program rewards applicants who abandoned an account opening, and nudges new accounts to complete funding.
- **Influencer program** for high-reach advocates.

### Leading Family Fintech App: In-App, Always Optimizing

This app's referral lives inside its mobile app and is triggered by in-app moments. It supports multiple advocate tiers.

- **Quarterly "boosted referrals."** Each quarter brings a boosted offer run as control vs. treatment.
- **First-referral bonus** to get new advocates over the line.
- **Partner and influencer codes.** A large library of promo-code offers for creators, schools, employers, affiliates and community groups.
- **Lifecycle offers.** Registration-abandon, upgrade and reactivation offers, coordinated with the CRM platform.

### Premium Furniture Retailer: Bursts, Nominations and Product Rewards

This retailer pairs an evergreen double-sided offer with a full calendar of promotions, timed to its biggest selling seasons and gifting holidays.

- **Frequent bursts.** Boosted advocate offers run several times a year, including January, spring, late summer and the holidays.
- **Rewards for sharing, not just sales.** In some bursts, advocates earn a reward when a friend signs up and a larger one when the friend buys, with caps to control cost.
- **Product rewards.** Some promotions reward advocates with a free accessory instead of a discount.
- **Holiday nominations.** Customers nominate moms, dads and everyday heroes for a chance to win a product, timed to Mother's Day, Father's Day, the fall and the holidays.
- **Sweepstakes on the base offer.** Holiday referrals earn entries to win a product.
- **Personal share links in email.** Bursts are promoted through the retailer's own email platform with each advocate's unique share link, so customers can share in one click.
- **Margin protection.** Friend offers carry a minimum purchase.

## Strategies to Implement

These eleven strategies turn the best-in-class practices into concrete moves. They are ordered roughly by how much contribution each can add, and they apply in any industry.

### 1. Build an Advocate Portfolio, Not One Program

The biggest single driver of contribution rate is the number of advocate audiences with a program built for them. Keep your core customers as the base, then add:

- [Employees](/product/product-overview/programs/refer-a-friend/employee-referral-program), especially frontline, store and field staff who talk to prospects every day.
- Business customers referring other businesses, with a B2B-appropriate offer.
- Partners and [ambassadors](/product/product-overview/programs/influencer) who meet your prospects at the moment of decision, such as creators and stylists, property managers, realtors or community organizations.
- Super advocates, your most active referrers, with personalized links and richer rewards.
- Affinity groups such as military, first responders, educators and students, each with its own offer and creative.

### 2. Make the Offer Match the Product and the Moment

Leaders tailor the offer to what is being bought: distinct offers and creative for each product or line of business, and a friend offer that is easy to understand at checkout or sign-up. A double-sided offer (advocate and friend both rewarded) is the norm among top programs. Retailers often set a minimum purchase on the friend offer to protect margin, and some reward advocates in product rather than discounts.

### 3. Run an Evergreen Offer Plus a Promotional Calendar

Keep a stable always-on offer so advocates know what to expect. Then plan four to six bursts a year, or more, around the natural demand peaks in your category:

- Seasonal peaks when your customers are most likely to buy or switch, such as Black Friday and Cyber Week, back to school, moving season or tax time.
- End-of-quarter pushes aligned to sales targets.
- Holidays and brand moments, such as Mother's Day, Father's Day, a customer appreciation day or a new collection launch.

### 4. Treat Every Burst as a Test

Split each promotion into control and treatment groups, and test the advocate offer and the friend offer separately. Candidates to test include a boosted advocate reward, a richer friend offer, sweepstakes entries, and urgency messaging. Keep what wins as the new baseline.

### 5. Add Gamification for Your Best Advocates

A small share of advocates drives a large share of referrals. Reward them for staying active:

- Milestone bonuses at set referral counts.
- A first-referral bonus to convert new advocates.
- Rewards for sharing, such as a small reward when a friend signs up and a larger one when the friend buys.
- Product rewards, such as a free accessory, which can feel more valuable than a discount of the same cost.
- [Sweepstakes](/product/product-overview/programs/sweepstakes) and seasonal prizes for shares and successful referrals.
- An ambassador tier with personalized links for community leaders and high-reach customers.

### 6. Put Referral Inside the Account and the App

The strongest programs don't rely on a landing page alone. Embed the advocate experience in your app and account portal, recognize logged-in customers automatically, and add referral entry points after key moments: a first purchase, a delivery, a five-star review, an upgrade, a renewal, or a high satisfaction score.

### 7. Use Lifecycle Messaging to Ask at the Right Time

- Post-purchase and post-upgrade asks, when satisfaction is highest.
- Share reminders to advocates who shared but have not yet converted a friend.
- Win-back offers for advocates who referred before but have gone quiet.
- Friend nurture and abandonment offers for referred prospects who started but did not finish.
- Reliable delivery. Send reward and status notifications through servicing channels so they reach the customer.

### 8. Capture Referrals Offline

If sales close in stores, showrooms, branches, over the phone or in the field, give those teams a simple way to capture the referring customer, such as [Go Extole](/product/product-overview/programs/refer-a-friend/go-extole). Retailers should also let referred friends redeem their offer in store as well as online. Then make sure those referrals are credited even when purchase and fulfillment events arrive out of sequence.

### 9. Promote It Like a Product

Referral only grows when customers see it. Put it in every owned channel on a regular rhythm: email and newsletters, the app, statements and bills, order confirmations, the website, social, stores and agent scripts. Give each burst its own launch plan, just as you would for a new product.

### 10. Put Referral Data to Work

Pull Extole data into your own BI, CRM and marketing systems. Report referral contribution alongside paid and other channels, and use the data to find your best advocates, target the right customers with the ask, time your offers and decide what to test next.

### 11. Reward Actions Beyond Referral

The leading programs use the same platform to reward the other actions that make a customer more valuable. These [reward-for-action and offer programs](/product/product-overview/programs/offer/index) build the engaged base that later refers:

- Write a review or upload a photo after purchase.
- Download the app or make a first in-app purchase.
- Join the loyalty program, complete a profile, or opt in to email and SMS.
- Complete a key step, such as funding an account, adding a second product or finishing an abandoned application.
- [Nominate](/product/product-overview/programs/nomination) someone for a seasonal giveaway, such as a hero, a mom or a dad.
- [Drop a hint](/product/product-overview/programs/drop-a-hint) about a product a customer wants as a gift, so friends and family get a nudge to buy it.

Each action can carry its own reward and messaging, and each one creates a new moment to ask for a referral. For why layering programs works, see [Acquisition, Retention, Engagement: Why Clients Need More Than Referral](/product/best-practices/program-strategy/why-clients-need-more-than-referral).

# Overview

These actions apply to every industry playbook. They cover how customers discover your program, how advocates share, how friends convert, which audiences to activate next, and which events to measure. Apply them alongside the industry playbook that matches your conversion journey — start from [Referral Program Growth Playbooks by Industry](/guides/strategy-and-best-practices/referral-growth-playbooks/index).

## Build the Measurement Foundation First

Before launching new campaigns, confirm that the program records the milestones that determine success in your business. A complete event model makes optimization more precise and prevents teams from treating a click, a signup, and a qualified customer as the same outcome.

At minimum, capture:

1. Share experience viewed or opened.
2. Advocate identified, when permitted.
3. Share completed, including source and share channel.
4. Friend click.
5. Offer claim or signup.
6. Business-specific qualification events.
7. Reward issued, delivered, redeemed, reversed, or expired.
8. Revenue or value associated with the qualified event.
9. Online, app, phone, store, branch, agent, or sales-assisted source.

Use consistent event names and dimensions across campaigns. Keep program-level reporting separate from campaign-level tests so temporary bursts do not obscure evergreen health.

Confirm every call-to-action passes its correct source parameter before you draw conclusions about which placements drive sharing. When one catch-all source dominates share reporting, placement-level optimization is guesswork — audit the tagging first, then decide what to add, move, or remove.

Track two comparisons alongside the funnel itself:

- **Sharing depth.** Alongside the count of advocates who share, track shares per advocate and friend clicks per share. A shrinking advocate base that shares more widely and converts more friends may indicate that the remaining advocates are highly motivated and that the program's real gap is recruitment rather than activation. Check it before acting on it: the same aggregate appears when a source of low-intent advocates simply stopped, so compare cohorts over time and split by acquisition source before moving budget from activation to recruitment. The topline share rate hides both signals.
- **Cost and contribution against other channels.** Report cost per acquired customer next to the same figure for your paid channels, so the program's efficiency case is made in numbers the wider team already uses. Pair it with contribution rate — referred new customers as a share of all new customers — and compare that with what strong programs in your category achieve.

## Establish a Placement System, Not a Single Referral Page

A referral page is a destination. A placement system is how customers discover it.

Use four complementary placement types:

- **Always-on placements:** global navigation, account menus, dashboards, product pages, app home tiles, logged-in experiences, and persistent banners.
- **Lifecycle-triggered placements:** purchase confirmation, activation, installation, funding, delivery, renewal, successful-service milestones, and post-experience surveys.
- **Outbound promotion:** dedicated email or SMS, lifecycle-message modules, push notifications, newsletters, and campaign reminders.
- **Assisted and offline promotion:** branch or store materials, QR codes, receipts, packaging inserts, employee signatures, field-team tools, and unique personal links.

The strongest placement mix combines persistent access with high-intent moments. Do not rely only on a footer link or a single launch email.

Judge each placement on its absolute output — traffic, advocates, and qualified friends — not only on its conversion or advocacy rate. A high-volume placement such as purchase confirmation shows the offer to every buyer, so it can depress the measured advocacy rate while producing most of your shares and referred revenue. Before you remove or rework a placement whose rate looks weak, check what share of total program output it carries.

Extend the system to people who are not customers yet. Presenting the program on your public marketing site recruits future advocates before their first purchase and makes the program part of the value proposition of joining. For placement and promotion tactics, see [How to Market Your Program](/guides/strategy-and-best-practices/tactics-and-strategy/how-to-market-your-program) and [Top-Performing CTA: Implement the Overlay Call-to-Action Across Your Site](/guides/strategy-and-best-practices/tactics-and-strategy/top-performing-cta-implement-the-overlay-call-to-action-across-your-site).

## Optimize the Advocate Experience

### Make the Proposition Unmistakable

State what the advocate receives, what the friend receives, and what must happen before either reward is earned. Use explicit labels such as "Refer a friend" instead of branded language that hides the program's purpose.

Name the reward itself in your calls-to-action; the reward, not the program name, is the hook. Test reward-led copy against the explicit label as a baseline. State the per-referral reward, any limit on what an advocate can earn, and the qualification requirements in plain language — advocates respond better when the earning ceiling is visible than when it sits in the terms.

For limited-time campaigns, distinguish the campaign reward from the evergreen reward and show the end date or campaign window.

### Reduce Sharing Friction

- Keep the primary share action visible above the fold on mobile.
- Offer copy-link and code sharing when both can be honored.
- Prioritize the share channels customers actually use, and remove the channels that produce few shares and few conversions so advocates funnel into the ones that work. Let your own channel data decide what stays enabled.
- Make default SMS and social messages action-oriented and specific to the brand, product, and offer. See [Delivering Share Messages](/guides/platform-overview/implementing-your-referral-program/technical-items/delivering-share-messages).
- Test the friend-facing share message when advocates share but friends do not click: the subject line, the strength of its call-to-action, the advocate's name in the subject so it reads as a note from a friend, and the friend's reward in the social share image.
- Test an embedded or in-app share experience against a pop-up or standalone landing page.
- Prevent other overlays from covering the share experience.
- Send reward reminders when earned rewards might otherwise go unused.
- Surface unused rewards inside the share experience when your overlay slot is already promoting another priority, so the advocate sees unredeemed value without a competing overlay.

### Use Incentives as a Testing Variable

Before you conclude the offer is right, compare it with what similar programs in your category give advocates and friends. An offer that reads as unexciting next to the category norm suppresses sharing before any creative fix can help. Validate a richer offer with a short burst or an A/B test before committing to a permanent change.

Test reward structure only after confirming visibility and flow quality. Useful tests include:

- A temporary reward burst against the evergreen offer.
- Symmetric rewards compared with an advocate-heavy or friend-heavy structure.
- Account credit, discount, gift card, points, partner benefit, free service, or product reward.
- A fixed reward compared with sweepstakes entries.
- A single reward compared with tiered milestones.
- Partner, local, or non-monetary benefits where a richer cash reward does not fit the economics.

Evaluate both conversion lift and cost. A higher reward that attracts lower-quality outcomes may reduce program value. For offer design, see [Create a Compelling Offer Structure](/guides/strategy-and-best-practices/tactics-and-strategy/create-a-compelling-offer-structure).

## Optimize the Friend Experience

The friend's experience should feel like a personal introduction, not an unrelated acquisition page.

- Match the landing page to the product, plan, account, itinerary, or use case that was shared.
- Explain that the visitor was referred and restate the benefit and qualification rules.
- Use deep links when the app or site supports them.
- Remove optional email capture when it does not serve a clear lifecycle or consent objective.
- Capture the friend's email deliberately when it does serve one. Emailing the offer gives the friend a durable copy that survives a closed tab, and ties the referral to an identifiable person for cleaner attribution. Track delivery rather than assuming it — mail bounces, filters to spam, and is mistyped. And treat delivering the offer and marketing to that person as two separate permissions: an address collected to send one requested offer is not consent to send reminders or campaigns, which need their own notice and opt-in under your privacy policy and the applicable marketing rules.
- Keep required fields and intermediate pages to a minimum.
- Use reminder messages for started but incomplete journeys.
- Track all qualification paths, including phone, store, branch, agent, and sales-assisted conversion. See [Taking Referrals from Site to Store](/guides/strategy-and-best-practices/tactics-and-strategy/taking-referrals-from-site-to-store) and [Capturing In-Person Referrals](/guides/strategy-and-best-practices/tactics-and-strategy/capturing-in-person-referrals).

## Use Audience Programs to Move Beyond the Evergreen Offer

The evergreen program should serve most eligible customers. Targeted campaigns can then create additional relevance without changing the base offer for everyone.

Useful audiences include:

- New customers after their first successful experience.
- Past advocates who have not shared recently.
- Super advocates who may respond to milestones or recognition.
- High-value, high-satisfaction, or high-engagement customers.
- Customers associated with a product, geography, branch, store, property, account type, or service tier.
- Employees, agents, advisors, partners, and field teams.
- Customers approaching a renewal, repeat purchase, trip, or other meaningful lifecycle event.

Measure targeted campaigns against a comparable audience or the audience's own prior behavior.

## Operating Cadence for Continuous Improvement

### Monthly

- Review placement coverage and source performance.
- Compare advocate, share, friend, and qualification funnels.
- Inspect mobile experiences and high-volume paths.
- Review reward delivery, redemption, reversals, and support issues.
- Identify one friction test and one distribution opportunity.

### Quarterly

- Compare program contribution and economics with prior periods.
- Refresh creative, default share messages, and key landing pages.
- Re-evaluate super, past, and new-customer audiences.
- Run or plan one targeted campaign or program-pattern test. See [Referral Program Patterns and Campaign Ideas](/guides/strategy-and-best-practices/referral-growth-playbooks/referral-program-patterns).
- Audit online, app, offline, and assisted-channel attribution.
- Review quality rules, legal, privacy, and operational controls.

### Annually

- Revisit the qualified outcome and reward economics.
- Review the program architecture by industry, product, region, and channel.
- Retire duplicate or low-value campaigns.
- Update integration, event, and data-quality requirements.
- Build the next year's testing calendar around business seasonality and customer moments.

# Overview

These patterns adapt to different industries. Match the conversion event, reward timing, eligibility, and measurement to your business model — the industry playbooks in [Referral Program Growth Playbooks by Industry](/guides/strategy-and-best-practices/referral-growth-playbooks/index) show which patterns fit each conversion journey.

## Classic Refer A Friend

**Use when:** You need an evergreen acquisition program with a simple give/get structure.

**Design:** Reward the advocate after the friend completes the qualifying event. Give the friend a clear welcome benefit. Keep the program visible year-round and refresh creative and messaging periodically.

**Measure:** Participation, share rate, share-message click-through, qualification rate, cost per acquired customer, revenue or value, and performance by placement.

## Reward Burst

**Use when:** You want a short-term lift, need to test a richer offer, or have a seasonal or capacity-driven objective.

**Design:** Increase or change the reward for a defined window. Target audiences with demonstrated likelihood to share. Make the campaign window and qualification rules explicit. See [Trigger Advocacy with Seasonal and Burst Promotions](/guides/strategy-and-best-practices/trigger-advocacy-with-seasonal-and-burst-promotions) and [How to Run a Burst](/guides/programs-and-campaigns/managing-campaigns/how-to-run-a-burst).

**Measure:** Incremental shares and qualified outcomes compared with the evergreen baseline, reward cost, audience response, and post-campaign performance.

## Welcome Offer

**Use when:** You want to convert anonymous or newly identified visitors and create an addressable audience.

**Design:** Present a first-purchase or first-action offer through a site or app experience. Use consented email or SMS capture only when follow-up is part of the plan. Keep welcome-offer reporting separate from friend reporting.

The same offer infrastructure runs welcome offers for adjacent goals: product cross-sell, cost-saving behaviors such as paperless enrollment, or a new location opening. Chain the two deliberately — invite a newly welcomed customer into the referral program while the welcome experience is fresh, so acquisition feeds the advocate base.

**Measure:** Offer claims, first conversions, average order or contract value, total revenue, and follow-up conversion.

## Bankable Loyalty

**Use when:** Customers can earn value across multiple actions or redeem accumulated rewards later.

**Design:** Award credit, points, or other stored value for qualifying actions. Show the balance and make redemption rules clear. Consider different earning rates for actions with different business value.

**Measure:** Enrollment, earning activity, redemption, breakage, repeat behavior, retained customers, and incremental value from rewarded actions.

## Employee Ambassador

**Use when:** Employees, branch staff, store associates, field technicians, or agents regularly interact with prospective customers.

**Design:** Give each employee a personal link, code, card, or QR experience. Make enrollment and sharing easy on mobile. Define whether the employee receives a reward, recognition, or team credit.

For a limited-time variant, invite employees to share an exclusive offer with a capped number of friends and family, with each friend receiving the offer by email. The cap keeps the richer offer controlled, and the exclusivity is what makes the share feel personal. The same capped structure works for your best customers as a low-risk way to test a richer offer on the people most willing to share.

**Measure:** Eligible employees, enrolled employees, active ambassadors, referrals per employee, qualified outcomes, source location, and quality compared with digital acquisition.

## Employee Program Refresh

**Use when:** An existing employee program has low awareness, low enrollment, or a large inactive population.

**Design:** Run a limited-time enrollment or reactivation campaign. Use internal communications, employee portals, manager reinforcement, and recognition for top contributors. Avoid rules that encourage low-quality referrals.

**Measure:** New enrollment, reactivated employees, active sharing, qualified outcomes, and sustained activity after the campaign.

## Sweepstakes

**Use when:** You want broad participation, list growth, customer insight, or a fixed incentive budget.

**Design:** Give entries for eligible actions such as sharing, referring, purchasing, or completing a profile. Keep the entry rules and winner process clear. Confirm legal, privacy, and jurisdiction requirements before launch.

<Warning>
  If any entry is earned by purchasing, United States law generally requires a free alternative method of entry that carries equal odds, stated alongside the paid route, together with official rules, eligibility, and the disclosure that no purchase is necessary and a purchase does not improve the chance of winning. Requirements vary by jurisdiction and by prize value. Have your legal or compliance team define the entry method and the rules before launch — this page is not legal advice.
</Warning>

**Measure:** Eligible participants, entries, new contacts with consent status, shares, qualified outcomes, acquisition cost, and post-campaign engagement.

## Tiered Rewards

**Use when:** A small group of customers can drive multiple high-quality referrals or actions.

**Design:** Increase rewards or recognition at meaningful milestones. Show progress and the next achievable level. Use caps and quality controls appropriate to the economics.

**Measure:** Advocates reaching each tier, referrals per advocate, marginal cost by tier, qualification quality, and repeat participation.

## Advocate Engagement and Referral Contests

**Use when:** A small group of advocates already drives a large share of referred conversions and you want to treat them as a managed audience rather than a happy accident.

**Design:** Layer engagement on top of the evergreen offer. Combine recognition and milestone rewards for active advocates, prompts for advocates who shared once and stopped, and a time-boxed contest with a prize for the advocate who drives the most qualified friends. Tie contest rules to qualified outcomes so the competition rewards quality rather than raw volume.

**Measure:** Active advocates, referrals per advocate, contest participation, qualified outcomes per participant, reactivated advocates, and sustained sharing after the campaign.

## Targeted Audience Campaign

**Use when:** A customer segment has a distinct value proposition, product, geography, lifecycle moment, or likelihood to advocate.

**Design:** Create segment-specific messaging, eligibility, landing page, and reward. Keep a control or baseline where possible. Avoid over-fragmenting into campaigns too small to evaluate.

**Measure:** Incremental participation, share and qualification lift, value per customer, and differences by segment.

## Nomination Campaign

**Use when:** You want to engage customers and deepen community relationships without asking anyone to buy.

**Design:** Invite customers to nominate someone — a community member, a local organization, or a person deserving recognition — alongside the transactional referral program. Collect consented first-party data about participants to inform later audience campaigns. Keep its reporting separate from the referral program so each is judged on its own goal.

**Measure:** Nominations submitted, participants and consent status, engagement with the winner announcement, new contacts added, and later participation in referral campaigns.

## Drop a Hint

**Use when:** A customer wants a specific product or experience as a gift and a traditional incentive is unnecessary or undesirable.

**Design:** Let the customer share the exact product or selection, including useful options such as size, color, date, or configuration. Preserve the selected product in the recipient's journey. See [Drop a Hint](/guides/programs-and-campaigns/asset-guides/drop-a-hint-asset-guide).

**Measure:** Hints sent, recipient engagement, product-level conversion, time to purchase, and products most frequently shared.

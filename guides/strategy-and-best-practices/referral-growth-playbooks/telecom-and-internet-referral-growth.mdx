# Overview

Use this playbook for internet service, wireless, streaming bundles, communications services, and other offerings where qualification may depend on geography, availability, credit, installation, activation, or a sales-assisted order.

Diagnose the program with the shared framework in [Referral Program Growth Playbooks by Industry](/guides/strategy-and-best-practices/referral-growth-playbooks/index) before choosing actions.

## Highest-Leverage Telecom Placements

### Post-Installation and Account Moments

- Ask for a referral after a successful installation or activation, when confidence is high.
- Add referral modules to installation confirmation, welcome, first-bill, and service milestone messages.
- Place a persistent referral entry on the authenticated dashboard, account menu, or mobile app.
- Use the login or account home screen for existing customers.
- Provide leave-behinds or a QR experience at installation when field teams are involved.

### Website and App

- Place referral invitations near service and product pages without interrupting availability checks.
- Build an integrated in-product or in-app share experience when a detached site creates friction.
- Support native sharing, QR codes, and a visible reward balance where appropriate.
- Use deep links to the correct service, wireless plan, bundle, or streaming offer.

### Field, Store, and Sales-Assisted Channels

- Give field technicians, store staff, agents, and sales teams personal links, codes, or QR materials.
- Capture referrals entered by phone or through a sales representative.
- Create simple mobile tools for field teams rather than requiring desktop access.
- Track the source employee, property, location, or partner while preserving customer consent and attribution rules.

## The Telecom Advocate Experience

- Explain whether the reward is earned at lead submission, order, installation, activation, or after a service period.
- Keep long qualification windows visible through status messaging and reward reminders.
- Make it easy to share a service address or general offer without exposing private account information.
- Use a persistent personal link or code that customers can retrieve from the account.
- Consider a reward bank when customers or employees may earn multiple rewards.

## The Telecom Friend Journey

- Send the friend into an availability or eligibility path that preserves the referral.
- Minimize duplicate data collection between referral, availability, order, and CRM forms.
- Tag the standard lead or order journey instead of forcing a separate referral-only form when possible.
- Explain installation, activation, service-area, and reward qualification requirements.
- Track each milestone separately: lead, qualified address, order, scheduled installation, completed installation, and active service.
- Support phone, store, agent, and field-assisted orders.

## Telecom Reward and Campaign Ideas

- Account credit, service credit, gift card, or partner benefit after activation.
- Smaller rewards for earlier verified actions when the full sales cycle is long.
- Reward bursts for specific products, geographies, or capacity goals.
- Separate home-internet, wireless, or streaming campaigns when the value proposition and qualification differ.
- Employee and field ambassador programs.
- Multi-dwelling-unit or property-specific campaigns.
- Sweepstakes for qualified lead or list growth, subject to legal review.

## Telecom Audience Expansion

Prioritize:

- Recently installed or activated customers.
- High-satisfaction and low-support-contact customers.
- Customers in serviceable areas with strong network capacity.
- Multi-dwelling-unit residents, property managers, or local communities.
- Field technicians, installers, store associates, and sales agents.
- Small-business customers and business partners.
- Existing customers eligible for an additional product or line.

## Telecom Measurement

Track:

- Referral leads and addresses submitted.
- Serviceability or eligibility.
- Orders, scheduled installations, completed installations, activation, and active-service qualification.
- Conversion and time between each milestone.
- Performance by product, geography, property, field employee, agent, store, and channel.
- Cost per qualified lead, installed customer, or active customer.
- Referral contribution to total acquisition and revenue.

## Telecom 30-60-90 Day Plan

### Days 1-30: Connect the Full Funnel

- Define lead, serviceability, order, installation, and activation events.
- Audit referral persistence across CRM, order, and installation systems.
- Add post-installation, account, and first-bill placements.
- Create a field or store tracking path.
- Clarify qualification and reward timing.

### Days 31-60: Remove Assisted-Channel Friction

- Tag the standard lead flow rather than duplicating forms.
- Add phone, agent, and store attribution.
- Test an integrated dashboard or app experience.
- Add reminder and status communication for long qualification windows.
- Review conversion drop-off by product and geography.

### Days 61-90: Expand Audiences

- Pilot an employee, field-agent, or multi-dwelling-unit program.
- Launch a product-specific or geography-specific burst.
- Compare acquisition quality and cost across digital and assisted sources.
- Create a repeatable playbook for the best-performing local channel.

## Common Telecom Pitfalls

- Rewarding at lead submission without controlling for quality.
- Losing referral attribution between the lead, CRM, order, and installation systems.
- Requiring friends to enter the same information more than once.
- Ignoring field, store, agent, or phone orders.
- Using one generic journey for products with different qualification rules.

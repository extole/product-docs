# Overview

Use this playbook when the qualifying event is an online or in-store purchase, fulfilled order, or another commerce event. It is especially relevant when customers browse multiple products, shop across web and app, or complete purchases through stores and call centers as well as ecommerce.

Diagnose the program with the shared framework in [Referral Program Growth Playbooks by Industry](/guides/strategy-and-best-practices/referral-growth-playbooks/index) before choosing actions.

## Highest-Leverage Retail Placements

### Website

- Trigger a referral invitation on the purchase-confirmation page after the transaction is complete.
- Keep an always-on link in the global navigation, customer account, or mobile menu.
- Test a product-page or category-page placement when product enthusiasm is high.
- Use an activity-based invitation after meaningful browsing rather than interrupting the first page view.
- Add a post-delivery or satisfaction placement when the customer has experienced the product.
- Check that a welcome-offer, email-capture, or consent pop-up does not obscure the share experience.

### Mobile App

- Add a persistent home tile or account-menu entry.
- Use push notifications for a new campaign and a measured evergreen cadence.
- Test an interstitial after repeat app engagement for customers who have not shared.
- Prefer an integrated in-app share experience when leaving the app creates friction.
- Deep link friends to the shared product, category, or relevant app screen.

### Email and SMS

- Send a dedicated referral message on a consistent cadence to eligible, engaged customers.
- Add a referral module to order confirmation, shipping, delivery, and post-purchase messages.
- Include the program in newsletters and major promotional campaigns without letting the referral CTA compete with a more urgent transactional action.
- Use reminders for advocates and friends who started but did not finish.

### Store and Fulfillment Channels

- Use QR codes or tracked links on receipts, packaging, inserts, displays, and associate materials.
- Give store associates or clienteling teams personal links when they are expected to promote the program.
- Honor referral codes or links for store and phone purchases, and send the conversion to the referral platform. See [Taking Referrals from Site to Store](/guides/strategy-and-best-practices/tactics-and-strategy/taking-referrals-from-site-to-store).
- Include a scannable or redeemable reward format when the reward must be used in-store.

## The Retail Advocate Experience

Retail customers often share a product, a deal, or a brand discovery. Make that context visible.

- Use clear refer-a-friend language and show both benefits near the primary CTA.
- Let customers copy a referral link or code.
- Optimize the default SMS and social message with a recognizable brand and a concrete customer benefit.
- Keep the experience compact on mobile and test embedded sharing against pop-up or standalone landing page formats.
- Send reminders for earned discounts, credits, or gift cards before they expire.

## The Retail Friend Journey

- Test the ecommerce homepage against a bestseller, category, or shared-product landing page.
- Use product deep links for product-led referrals and Drop a Hint.
- Carry the referral context and offer through product selection and checkout.
- Remove optional email capture when it causes avoidable abandonment.
- Confirm that discount stacking, exclusions, and minimum purchase rules are explained before checkout.
- Track referred purchases completed in a store, by phone, or through assisted selling.

## Retail Reward and Campaign Ideas

- Temporary reward bursts for seasonal launches or slower periods.
- Sweepstakes with fixed prizes such as a shopping experience or product collection.
- Tiered rewards for customers who refer multiple qualified purchasers.
- Product rewards or store-specific benefits when they create more excitement than a generic discount.
- Drop a Hint for gifts, registries, furniture, apparel, and other product-specific sharing.
- Loyalty actions for reviews, profile completion, repeat purchases, showroom visits, or other high-value behaviors.

See [Referral Program Patterns and Campaign Ideas](/guides/strategy-and-best-practices/referral-growth-playbooks/referral-program-patterns) for the full pattern library.

## Retail Audience Expansion

Prioritize:

- New customers after purchase or delivery.
- Customers with multiple purchases or high satisfaction.
- Past advocates who have not shared recently.
- Super advocates for tiered or recognition campaigns.
- App customers, loyalty members, store customers, and product-specific buyers.
- Store associates, stylists, designers, or clienteling teams.

## Retail Measurement

Track:

- Purchase-confirmation and post-delivery participation.
- Shares and qualified purchases by web, app, email, SMS, store, and phone.
- Product or category associated with the share.
- Friend conversion, order value, return or cancellation, and net revenue.
- Reward issuance and redemption.
- Cost per acquired customer and referral contribution to new-customer acquisition.
- Average order value and repeat-purchase behavior of referred friends compared with your overall customer base.

Where referred customers show a premium, use it to justify investing in advocate volume — the growth problem is reach, not quality. Where they do not, review offer targeting and quality rules before scaling.

## Retail 30-60-90 Day Plan

### Days 1-30: Distribution and Instrumentation

- Map every current placement and its share volume.
- Add purchase-confirmation, account, and mobile-menu placements if missing.
- Add referral modules to confirmation, shipping, delivery, and post-purchase messaging.
- Verify mobile rendering and pop-up priority.
- Confirm store and phone conversion tracking.

### Days 31-60: Funnel Optimization

- Rewrite advocate and friend copy for clarity.
- Test embedded sharing or a simplified share widget.
- Test homepage versus product or category landing pages.
- Enable reminder flows and review share-channel performance.
- Run one controlled reward or message test.

### Days 61-90: Scale and Audience Programs

- Launch a targeted campaign for past or super advocates.
- Add a store-associate or clienteling use case where relevant.
- Pilot a seasonal burst, tiered reward, sweepstakes, or Drop a Hint experience.
- Compare program contribution and economics before and after the changes.

## Common Retail Pitfalls

- Treating a footer link as sufficient promotion.
- Allowing another pop-up to cover the share experience.
- Sending every friend to a generic homepage.
- Ignoring store, phone, or assisted purchases.
- Raising the reward before fixing mobile or messaging friction.
- Using vague program labels that do not say "refer" or explain the benefit.

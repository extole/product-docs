# Overview

Use this playbook for banks, credit unions, fintechs, and other financial institutions where a friend may need to open an account, pass eligibility or identity checks, fund the account, maintain a balance, make a transaction, or complete another qualifying action.

Diagnose the program with the shared framework in [Referral Program Growth Playbooks by Industry](/guides/strategy-and-best-practices/referral-growth-playbooks/index) before choosing actions.

## Highest-Leverage Financial-Services Placements

### Online and Mobile Banking

- Add a persistent referral tile or banner on the authenticated home screen.
- Place the program in the account menu or member-services area.
- Use push notifications for a new campaign and a measured evergreen cadence.
- Test an interstitial after repeat logins for eligible members who have not shared.
- Integrate sharing into online or mobile banking when an external landing page breaks trust or continuity.

### Marketing Website

- Add a clear referral entry to global navigation or the mobile menu.
- Place contextual invitations on checking, savings, credit, loan, or membership pages.
- Use activity-based or exit-aware promotion carefully so it does not interrupt an application.
- Make eligibility and product qualification easy to understand before the friend starts.

### Lifecycle and Transactional Messaging

- Add referral modules to new-member onboarding, account-opening confirmation, funding, deposit, loan-payment, and satisfaction messages where appropriate.
- Send dedicated email or SMS to eligible members on a consistent but measured cadence.
- Use newsletters and e-statements to maintain awareness.
- Trigger referral asks after a positive service or product milestone.

### Branch and Employee Channels

- Use branch signage, take-home materials, ATM or receipt messaging, and QR experiences.
- Give employees personal links or codes when they are expected to promote the program. See [Capturing In-Person Referrals](/guides/strategy-and-best-practices/tactics-and-strategy/capturing-in-person-referrals).
- Use email signatures or employee portals to make the program easy to access.
- Consider branch-level recognition or contests based on qualified outcomes, not raw leads.

## The Financial-Services Advocate Experience

- State member eligibility and the exact qualifying product or action.
- Explain when the reward will be issued, including funding, balance, transaction, or waiting-period requirements.
- Keep the reward and requirement visible above the fold on mobile.
- Let members share through a personal link or code without exposing account data.
- Use status and reminder communication for long qualification windows.
- Make the program available to all intended member groups unless a product or regulatory rule requires segmentation.

## The Financial-Services Friend Journey

- Send the friend to the relevant account or product application.
- Preserve the referral through identity, application, funding, and qualification systems.
- Avoid a separate email-capture step when the standard application already captures permitted contact data.
- Explain membership eligibility, product requirements, funding, and reward timing before application.
- Support multiple qualifying products only when tracking and terms are unambiguous.
- Send reminders for incomplete applications or unfunded accounts when consent and policy allow.

## Financial-Services Reward and Campaign Ideas

- Cash, account credit, points, or gift card after a qualified account event.
- Product-specific campaigns for checking, savings, loans, cards, or business accounts.
- New-member onboarding campaigns that prompt advocacy after an early positive milestone.
- Employee ambassador and branch engagement programs.
- Targeted campaigns for members with high engagement or specific product relationships.
- [Bankable loyalty](/guides/strategy-and-best-practices/referral-growth-playbooks/referral-program-patterns) — accumulated credit or points across multiple qualifying actions, redeemed later.
- Reward bursts or sweepstakes after compliance and legal review.

## Financial-Services Audience Expansion

Prioritize:

- Newly onboarded and funded members after a positive first experience.
- Digitally engaged members who regularly use online or mobile banking.
- Members with high satisfaction or multiple products.
- Past advocates and super advocates.
- Employees and branch teams.
- Members eligible for a relevant cross-sell or new product.
- Geographic audiences around a new branch or market expansion.

## Financial-Services Measurement

Track:

- Participation by marketing site, online banking, mobile app, email, SMS, branch, and employee source.
- Application starts and completed applications.
- Approved, opened, funded, and fully qualified accounts.
- Time to funding and time to qualification.
- Product type, branch, employee, and member segment.
- Reward issuance, reversals, and cost per qualified account.
- Deposits, balances, transactions, revenue, or other approved value measures.

## Financial-Services 30-60-90 Day Plan

### Days 1-30: Define Qualification and Improve Access

- Document every event between application and qualified account.
- Add online-banking, mobile-app, marketing-site, and onboarding placements.
- Audit terms, mobile visibility, and qualification language.
- Confirm referral persistence through application and core systems.
- Build source reporting for branch and employee channels.

### Days 31-60: Reduce Application Friction

- Send friends directly to the relevant product application.
- Remove redundant capture steps.
- Add permitted reminders for incomplete or unfunded accounts.
- Test clearer copy before changing reward value.
- Launch a controlled product-specific or member-segment campaign.

### Days 61-90: Activate the Institution

- Pilot an employee or branch ambassador program.
- Add e-statement, newsletter, ATM, and branch promotion where appropriate.
- Introduce a targeted audience, bankable loyalty, or reward-burst campaign.
- Compare funded and qualified-account quality across sources.

## Common Financial-Services Pitfalls

- Measuring account opening when funding or another event defines value.
- Losing referral attribution during identity, application, or core-system handoffs.
- Hiding product, eligibility, funding, or waiting-period requirements.
- Treating branch and employee promotion as untracked awareness.
- Rewarding raw leads or applications without quality controls.
- Publishing a campaign before compliance, legal, privacy, and operational review.

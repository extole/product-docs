# Overview

Use this playbook when the referred outcome is a business lead, qualified meeting, opportunity, contract, or another sales-assisted event. It applies to software, professional services, education, local services, and other considered purchases with a multi-step sales cycle.

Diagnose the program with the shared framework in [Referral Program Growth Playbooks by Industry](/guides/strategy-and-best-practices/referral-growth-playbooks/index) before choosing actions.

## Highest-Leverage B2B Placements

### Product and Customer Experience

- Place a persistent referral entry in the product home, account menu, customer portal, or login experience.
- Trigger referral invitations after adoption milestones, successful outcomes, renewals, or executive business reviews.
- Use campaign banners or in-product messages for time-bound offers.
- Build an integrated share or introduction flow when leaving the product creates friction.

### Lifecycle and Customer Marketing

- Send dedicated referral messages after major success milestones.
- Add referral modules to newsletters, renewal communications, education, and advocacy messages.
- Segment by customer health, tenure, product adoption, satisfaction, and relationship strength.
- Use reminders for advocates who begin an introduction but do not complete it.

### Sales, Service, Partner, and Field Channels

- Give account teams, service professionals, partners, and field staff personal links or simple mobile tools.
- Provide an assisted introduction flow that can send a colleague's information to the CRM with appropriate consent.
- Use a standard lead form with referral tagging when a separate referral form would create duplicate work.
- Track offline or sales-entered referrals through the CRM or an authenticated referral page.

## The B2B Advocate Experience

- Explain what counts as a qualified outcome and whether the advocate is rewarded at lead, meeting, opportunity, or closed business.
- Use a guided introduction flow when a warm colleague-to-colleague handoff is more valuable than a broadcast share.
- Offer a personal link or code for scalable sharing while preserving an assisted option.
- Use higher-value or tiered rewards only when they fit policy, ethics, and sales-cycle economics.
- Consider non-cash recognition, charitable rewards, account benefits, or partner value where direct incentives are restricted.

## The B2B Referred-Prospect Journey

- Send the referred prospect to the most relevant product, use-case, industry, or service page.
- Preserve referral attribution through marketing automation, CRM, sales qualification, opportunity, and closed business.
- Avoid a second form when the existing lead flow can carry referral data.
- Keep requested information proportional to the stage of the relationship.
- Route high-intent warm introductions quickly and communicate follow-up expectations.
- Record disqualification reason and sales-cycle stage for quality analysis.

## B2B Reward and Campaign Ideas

- Fixed reward at qualified lead, completed meeting, opportunity, or closed business.
- Tiered rewards for multiple qualified outcomes.
- Smaller verified-action rewards for long sales cycles.
- Partner, reseller, service-provider, or customer-advisory programs.
- Employee and field-team ambassador programs.
- Sweepstakes for qualified list growth or event follow-up, subject to legal review.
- Reward campaigns for onboarding, usage, feedback, reviews, or expansion actions.

See [Referral Program Patterns and Campaign Ideas](/guides/strategy-and-best-practices/referral-growth-playbooks/referral-program-patterns) for the full pattern library.

## B2B Audience Expansion

Prioritize:

- Customers with demonstrated adoption and measurable success.
- Renewed or expanded customers.
- Executive sponsors, administrators, and power users.
- Past advocates and customers with strong professional networks.
- Partners, agencies, consultants, and service providers.
- Employees, sales teams, account teams, and field professionals.
- Customers in high-priority industries, regions, or use cases.

## B2B Measurement

Track:

- Referral invitations, shares, and assisted introductions.
- Leads, accepted leads, qualified meetings, opportunities, and closed business.
- Conversion and time between each sales stage.
- Pipeline and revenue influenced by referrals.
- Cost per lead, cost per opportunity, and cost per acquired customer.
- Source customer, advocate role, account team, partner, industry, region, and use case.
- Referred-account retention, expansion, and quality compared with other sources.

## B2B 30-60-90 Day Plan

### Days 1-30: Align Referral and CRM Stages

- Define the exact qualified outcome and reward event.
- Map referral data through marketing automation and CRM.
- Add product, portal, milestone, and customer-marketing placements.
- Create an assisted introduction path.
- Establish response-time and ownership rules for warm referrals.

### Days 31-60: Improve Relevance and Quality

- Send referred prospects to use-case or industry-specific landing pages.
- Remove duplicate forms and preserve referral data in the standard lead flow.
- Segment customers by adoption and success.
- Test one reward structure or message against a baseline.
- Review disqualification reasons and stage conversion.

### Days 61-90: Expand Trusted Networks

- Launch a partner, service-provider, employee, or field-team pilot.
- Add tiered or targeted campaigns for proven advocates.
- Compare pipeline quality, sales velocity, and cost with other sources.
- Operationalize the best-performing program as a repeatable sales and customer-success motion.

## Common B2B Pitfalls

- Rewarding raw lead volume without qualification controls.
- Losing attribution between marketing automation, CRM, and opportunity systems.
- Sending every prospect to a generic homepage.
- Asking customers to complete a cold lead form instead of enabling a warm introduction.
- Ignoring ethics, employer-gift, partner, or industry restrictions.
- Measuring leads without measuring pipeline, revenue, and customer quality.

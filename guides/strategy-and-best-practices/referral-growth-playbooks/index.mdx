# Overview

Referral programs grow when the program fits the way customers buy, use, and recommend a product. A retail referral may convert in one session. A telecom referral may require an availability check, a sales conversation, and an installation. A credit union referral may not qualify until a new account is funded. Treating these journeys as if they were the same creates unnecessary friction and misleading measurement.

Use these playbooks to identify the highest-leverage actions for your business model. Start with the shared growth framework on this page, then apply the industry playbook that most closely matches your conversion journey.

## A Shared Framework for Diagnosing Referral Growth

Analyze the program as four connected stages. Improve the earliest weak stage first, because increasing downstream conversion will not compensate for a program that customers cannot find.

Give each stage an explicit target and grade it, rather than judging the program on total revenue alone. Compare participation, share rate, friend conversion, and contribution with typical programs in your category, then direct the next quarter's work at the stage furthest below its target. Each stage has its own fix: a participation gap calls for more placements, a share gap for a clearer offer and share experience, and a friend-conversion gap for landing page and reminder work. Your Extole team can supply category reference points for each stage.

### 1. Discoverability and Participation

Ask whether eligible customers encounter a clear referral invitation at moments when they are likely to recommend the brand.

Review:

- Placement coverage across the website, app or authenticated experience, lifecycle messaging, and offline channels.
- The share of eligible customers who see or open a share experience.
- Participation by source, device, customer segment, and lifecycle stage.
- Conflicts with other overlays, welcome offers, consent prompts, or promotional banners.
- Mobile placement, rendering, and above-the-fold visibility.

A low participation rate is usually a distribution problem before it is an incentive problem. Add relevant placements before increasing reward cost.

### 2. Advocate Action

Ask whether customers who open the share experience understand the value and can share without friction.

Review:

- The share rate from share-experience visitors to completed shares.
- Shares per advocate and friend clicks per share, not only the number of advocates who share. A smaller advocate base that shares more widely tells you the gap is recruiting new sharers, not motivating existing ones.
- Reward clarity, qualification rules, expiration language, and brand voice.
- Whether the advocate can copy a link or code and use the share channels they prefer.
- The number of required fields and steps.
- Embedded or in-app sharing compared with a detached pop-up or standalone landing page.
- Follow-up reminders for advocates who opened the share experience but did not share.

A low share rate often indicates unclear messaging, excessive steps, an unconvincing advocate reward, or a poor mobile experience.

### 3. Friend Conversion

Ask whether the friend receives a warm, relevant introduction and can complete the intended action.

Review:

- Click-through from the share message.
- Referred-friend conversion compared with your paid and organic channels.
- Offer claim or signup rate.
- Each business-specific milestone, such as trial, purchase, funded account, qualified lead, booking, or installation.
- The landing page and whether it matches the message or product shared.
- Optional email capture or other steps that may interrupt the journey.
- Reminder messages for friends who started but did not qualify.
- Online and offline conversion capture.

A referred friend arrives with a warm introduction from someone they trust, so referral should convert above your other acquisition channels rather than merely match them. Treat parity or underperformance as evidence of friction in the friend journey.

A low conversion rate often calls for a better landing page, fewer fields, clearer requirements, stronger message continuity, or improved tracking — not a larger reward.

### 4. Scale and Business Contribution

Ask whether the program is reaching enough of the right customers and contributing meaningful acquisition or revenue.

Review:

- Friends or qualified outcomes as a share of total acquisition.
- Revenue, first-period revenue, funded balances, bookings, or pipeline influenced by referrals.
- Cost per acquired customer or cost per qualified outcome, reported next to the same figure for your paid channels.
- Contribution rate — referred new customers as a share of all new customers — compared with what strong programs in your category achieve.
- Repeat referral behavior and the contribution of super advocates.
- Performance by audience, placement, campaign, channel, geography, and product.
- Year-over-year and quarter-over-quarter changes.

Scale a program by expanding distribution and audiences while preserving conversion quality and unit economics.

# Overview

The Redemption Rate is the ratio of accumulated value to dollars, and it is the single number that
decides what your loyalty program costs and whether members think it is worth participating in. The
default is 1:1 — one point equals one dollar. A ten-point rate means a member earns one dollar for
every ten points.

There is no correct rate in the abstract. The rate that works is the one where the cost per earning
action is sustainable for you and the reward is reachable for the member. This page describes how
to get to that number.

# Work Backwards from the Reward

Start at the end. Decide what the first meaningful reward is and what it costs you, then work back
to the earning actions.

1. **Pick the first reward a member should reach.** Something they would genuinely want — a ten
   dollar coupon, a free product, a shipping credit.
2. **Establish what it costs you.** Use the marginal cost, not the retail price. A ten dollar
   coupon on a product with a 60 percent margin costs you four dollars.
3. **Decide how long it should take.** Fast enough that a member believes the program works, slow
   enough that the reward feels earned. For most programs the first reward should be reachable
   within a few normal interactions, not one, and not twenty.
4. **Divide.** The points earned across those interactions, set against the reward's point cost,
   gives you the rate.

# Set the Cost per Action

Once you have the rate, price each way to earn against it.

Purchases are usually the anchor. If a program awards points as a percentage of spend, that
percentage is a direct discount on every transaction, and it is worth calculating what it costs at
your current volume before launch rather than after.

Non-purchase actions are priced differently. A review or a profile completion has no transaction
value, so price it against what the action is worth to you — the conversion lift from having
reviews, or the targeting value of the data.

<Warning>
  Price every dynamic reward with a maximum per transaction. Without a deliberate cap, an unusually
  large order issues an unusually large reward, and the exposure scales with your best customers'
  order values.
</Warning>

# Make the Rate Legible

A rate members cannot do arithmetic on is a rate they will not chase.

* **Round numbers beat precise ones.** Ten points per dollar is easier to hold in your head than 7.5.
* **State the reward, not just the rate.** "500 points gets you a ten dollar coupon" tells a member more than "10 points per dollar".
* **Keep one rate where you can.** A single rate across earning actions is easier to communicate than a different rate for each.

# Decide the First Threshold

The minimum redemption amount decides how long a member holds a balance before they can do anything
with it.

Set it too low and members redeem constantly for trivial value, and the program costs you a
transaction fee each time without building any habit. Set it too high and members give up before
reaching it, leaving you with a growing balance of points that were never worth anything to
anybody.

The minimum and maximum are configured as value limits alongside the rate. Setting a limit to `0`
disables it.

# Watch What Happens After Launch

The rate you launch with is a hypothesis. Check it against these:

* **Redemption rate.** What proportion of issued points get spent. A low figure means the threshold is out of reach or the rewards are not wanted.
* **Time to first redemption.** How long a member takes to reach their first reward. If it is far longer than you designed for, the rate is too thin.
* **Outstanding balance.** The total unredeemed. It should stabilize rather than climb without limit.
* **Cost per earning action.** What each way to earn is actually costing, against what you modeled.

See [Reward Bank Reports](/guides/dashboards-and-reporting/report-types/rewards-reports/reward-bank-reports).

# Changing the Rate Later

Changing the Redemption Rate changes the value of points members already hold, so treat it as a
policy change rather than a configuration tweak.

Before changing a live rate:

* Check what your program terms say about changes to point value.
* Prefer changing what points are worth going forward over revaluing existing balances.
* Consider adjusting earn rates on individual actions instead, which leaves the value of held points untouched.
* Tell members before it takes effect.

<Tip>
  Test a rate change on a variant campaign before rolling it out. See

</Tip>
